ABSTRACT 

Model compression strategies are widely employed to reduce memory footprint and network complexity, particularly for devices with constrained computational, memory, and energy resources. Prior works that rely on simultaneous conversion from floating-point high-precision (FP32) to integer low-precision (INT4) repre-sentations and distillation into smaller models suffer from unstable training and drastic degradation of prediction performance. To ad-dress these limitations, we propose a unified framework, known as 

Progressive Multi-Ancestor Bit-depth Distillation (PMABD), that progressively compresses the network while transferring knowl-edge through a growing pool of higher-precision ancestor teachers. PMABD generates a sequence of intermediate teachers that each learn from all higher-precision ancestors and jointly supervise the final target student. This multi-ancestor, multi-stage design sta-bilizes ultra-low-bit quantization by lowering quantization noise profiles across training and ensuring stable quantization. Experi-ments on CIFAR-10/100 with ResNet-20/32/18, and Tiny-ImageNet with MobileNetV2 show that PMABD outperforms state-of-the-art compression frameworks, results in 1.06 % increase in performance of W2A2 (ResNet-18/CIFAR-100) student model. We show that a saturation-based stopping criterion contributes to improve the performance of our final student. 

Index Terms — Distillation, Quantization, Low-bit Network, Ancestor Model Compression 

1. INTRODUCTION 

Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) have demonstrated remarkable per-formance for various real-world applications, including image clas-sification, object detection, and speech recognition [1]. However, with ever-increasing model depth and complexity, deploying deep learning models has been a significant challenge, particularly for resource-constrained edge devices [2, 3]. This motivates us to ex-plore model compression approaches so that DNNs can execute on edge devices with minimal accuracy degradation. Recent works have explored techniques to obtain low-bit target student networks through knowledge distillation [4, 5]. Frameworks that reduce precision directly from FP32 to INT4 drastically degrade overall performance and introduce substantial quantization error. Al-though progressive quantization (FP32 →INT8 →INT4) shows im-proved results over direct reduction [6, 7], the model’s performance monotonically decays, and it is difficult to recover accuracy at sub-sequent lower precision levels. Multiple and intermediate teachers have been extensively used to provide flexible and appropriate guid-ance for improved knowledge distillation [8, 9, 10, 11]. For model compression, recent advanced approaches use dis-tillation to train low-precision student networks [12, 13, 14]. QKD does not ex-plicitly model the progressive reduction of precision levels, which can lead to unstable optimization and increased quantization noise. CMT-KD aggregates knowledge from multiple quantized teachers through learned importance weights [4]. It treats teachers as inde-pendent knowledge sources and jointly optimizes multiple teachers and students within a single training stage. As a result, the knowl-edge transfer process does not explicitly model how representations evolve across precision levels, and substantial distribution shifts arise between high-precision and ultra-low-bit networks. Divide-and-Conquer Quantization (DCQ) partitions teachers into several sections, each of which trains the student using its inter-mediate features; the sections are then combined to form a complete low-bit student model [16]. Quantized Feature Distillation (QFD) distills knowledge from the features of a quantized teacher by align-ing the teacher network with the limited representational capacity of the student model, and demonstrates performance improvement over conventional knowledge distillation [5]. SQAKD is a frame-work that reformulates QAT as a co-optimization problem that jointly minimizes the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between full-precision and low-bit model outputs and the discretization error, without supervision from labels [17]. Unified Progressive Quantiza-tion (UPQ) introduces a sequential quantization pipeline in which an intermediate INT4 PTQ model serves as a warm-start initialization for subsequent INT2 quantization [13]. However, the intermediate INT4 model is used only for initialization, while knowledge distilla-tion is performed solely from the single original FP16 teacher to the arXiv:2610.04100v1 [cs.LG] 2 Oct 2026 INT2 student. The primary contributions of this work are as follows: • We propose a novel multi-stage distillation framework that progressively lowers the precision of high-precision mod-els by accumulating the knowledge of all ancestor models. Leveraging ancestor knowledge allows the framework to recover information lost during distillation and achieve bet-ter training stability throughout the quantization and yield a smoother optimization landscape for the student model. • After a complete training run, the framework produces mul-tiple well-performing models at different precision levels, available for deployment across devices with varied compute and memory requirements. • Extensive empirical evaluation comparing the PMABD framework against standard optimization techniques on ResNet-32/20/18, and MobileNetV2. PMABD is an ar-chitecture agnostic framework; it is compatible with any CNN architecture and supports cross-architecture training at any precision stage. Results demonstrate that progres-sive multi-ancestor supervision yields higher accuracy and stability. 

2. METHOD 

Progressive Multi-Ancestor Bit-Depth Distillation (PMABD) frame-work is formulated as a progressive loop that generates lower-precision student models by aggregating knowledge of the variable-precision teachers from the model pool, as illustrated in Fig. 1. Given a frozen teacher T1 at certain high precision b1, our ob-jective is to achieve a Tk model having precision bk (bk < b i ∀ i < k). Here, all ancestor teachers {T1, T 2, . . . , T k−1} from the model pool distill their knowledge to Tk model. We can consider two suc-cessive teachers can have the same precision b1 ≥ b2 ≥ · · · ≥ bk

albeit b1 > b k its hold for our desired output model. The models at specific precision bi are denoted by Mi.

2.1. Model Architecture 

PMABD employs a three distinct model level, where each model level fulfills a distinct role in the knowledge distillation and precision reduction process: 

1 Teacher Models - PMABD requires at least one high-capacity frozen FP32 teacher ( M1) to anchor the distillation chain, which provides high representational capacity and serves as a stable semantic anchor throughout all subsequent stages. 

2 Intermediate Models - Intermediate models serve the dual purpose of being trained students and, once training is complete, contribute as a teacher in distillation chain. The architecture of the intermediate models can be chosen independently from the teacher(s) or the final student. 

3 Student Model - The final student is the target low-bit ( bk )model to be deployed. In our proposed framework its architecture is independent, no architectural constraint, of teacher and intermediate models. The framework begins with a pretrained FP32 teacher model added to an initially empty pool ( P). The loop proceeds by selecting a target bit-depth lower than any existing model in the pool, train-ing a new student at that bit-depth under supervision from all current pool members, and then inserting the trained student into the pool 

P ← P ∪ { Tk }. This process repeats until the target precision is reached. Each teacher distills its logits and features to successive stu-dent model until patience-based early stopping criteria is achieved. The specific precision ladder, number of stages, and architecture at each stage are configuration choices, not structural requirements of the framework. However, the criteria to identify our required stu-dent model at a desired precision is decided based on the saturation condition, which is verified using accuracy ( Tk ) ≤ accuracy ( Tk−1), if it is fulfilled, we treat Tk−1 as our desired student model. A tie means the new stage brings no gain, so we stop and keep the earlier, cheaper model.

4.3. Ablation on Intermediate Teachers 

To check that every intermediate precision contributes, we train the ResNet-18/CIFAR-100 W2A2 student with progressively larger teacher pools. Each run warm-starts from the lowest-precision model in its pool. As Table 7 shows, test accuracy rises with each teacher added, and the full pool beats distillation from the FP32 teacher alone by 1.33 points. No single stage accounts for the gain; each intermediate model adds to it. 

5. CONCLUSION 

We presented Progressive Multi-Ancestor Bit-depth Distillation (PMABD) , a unified framework that jointly addresses network quan-tization and knowledge distillation through progressive multi-stage compression. By constructing a sequence of intermediate teachers and enabling each stage to learn from all higher-precision ancestors, PMABD effectively reduces the knowledge gap between teacher and student, leading to more stable optimization during ultra-low-bit quantization. Evaluations on CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny-Imagenet dataset across multiple architectures demonstrate that PMABD achieves superior accuracy while significantly reducing model com-plexity and memory footprint, outperforming existing SOTA meth-ods. These results suggest that progressive multi-ancestor knowl-edge transfer is a promising direction for deploying deep neural networks on resource-constrained edge devices. 6. REFERENCES
