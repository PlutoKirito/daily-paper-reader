## ABSTRACT 

Recent world action models (WAMs) reuse pretrained video VAEs whose encoder latents directly condition downstream action policies. Quantization must there-fore preserve not only reconstruction fidelity but also the policy-facing latent contract expected by the frozen policy. Direct NVFP4 leaves W4A4 quantiza-tion error uncompensated, whereas joint quantization-aware training (QAT) can recover reconstruction by moving this representation. On Wan2.1, joint QAT nearly matches FP32 VBench-7 (0.7403 versus 0.7409), yet LIBERO success collapses from 95 .5% to 10 .5% . Controlled decoder-only experiments show that activation quantize–dequantize operations alter the reconstruction signal and decoder Jaco-bian, redirecting the gradient returned to the encoder and inducing persistent latent drift. Based on this mechanism, we introduce LatentQuant , a two-stage NVFP4 QAT framework that first aligns the quantized encoder with its high-precision counterpart, then freezes it while adapting the decoder. Across Wan2.1 and Wan2.2, LatentQuant preserves near-baseline control and high reconstruction quality, achiev-ing 95 .75% success on LIBERO and 68 .8% on RoboTwin. On NVIDIA B300 GPUs, NVFP4 execution achieves 1.17×–1.26× end-to-end VAE speedups over BF16 cuDNN 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Variational autoencoders (VAEs) are conventionally evaluated by reconstruction fidelity (Yu et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2024; NVIDIA et al., 2025). In recent world action models (WAMs), however, the encoder representation can also be consumed directly by a downstream action policy (Cheang et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025b; 2026a; Ye et al., 2026). Closed-loop control further imposes a tight per-step latency budget, making the VAE worth optimizing even when its cost is secondary in offline video generation (Akbari et al., 2026; Xu et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026a). The latent is therefore both an internal code and a policy-facing representation whose semantics must remain compatible with a pretrained policy, a requirement not captured by reconstruction-centric quantization. We call this requirement the policy-facing latent contract . Reconstruction constrains the compo-sition of an encoder E and decoder D, but not their intermediate coordinates. For any invertible transformation T ,

E′(x) = T (E(x)) , D′(z) = D(T −1(z)) , (1) the transformed pair preserves reconstruction, whereas a fixed policy P is generally not invariant to the same reparameterization:

Reconstruction quality can thus conceal severe incompatibility with the frozen policy. Controlled interventions trace this mismatch to decoder activation quantization. By changing both the reconstruction signal and the decoder Jacobian, quantization redirects the gradient returned to the encoder; repeated optimizer updates consolidate this signal into a persistent latent shift. The failure is therefore a learned drift of the policy-facing representation, not merely an instantaneous perturbation at the encoder output. This analysis suggests a hard design constraint: reconstruction adaptation should not rewrite an estab-lished latent contract. Across Wan2.1 and Wan2.2 (Wan Team, 2025), LatentQuant retains near-baseline control and high reconstruction quality: 95 .75% versus 95 .50% success on LIBERO, and 68 .8% versus 69 .8% on RoboTwin (Chen et al., 2025b). A native packed-NVFP4 convolution backend on NVIDIA B300 GPUs further demonstrates that contract preservation is compatible with end-to-end VAE acceleration. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We formulate the policy-facing latent contract for quantizing VAEs consumed by fixed downstream policies, and show that reconstruction fidelity can conceal severe policy incom-patibility. • We identify an optimization-induced latent-contract violation mechanism under joint low-bit QAT, showing that decoder-side quantization redirects reconstruction gradients returned to the encoder and consolidates them into persistent learned latent drift. • We propose LatentQuant , a two-stage QAT method which separates quantized encoder alignment from decoder adaptation to preserve the established latent contract. On NVIDIA 2B300 GPUs, NVFP4 execution achieves 1.17 ×–1.26 × end-to-end VAE speedups over BF16 cuDNN. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

WAMs and policy-consuming latents. Recent video-action models couple latent visual representa-tions with action prediction through diverse architectures. A growing class of WAMs builds directly on latent video generators and inherits their pretrained VAEs. GR-2 jointly predicts future videos and actions, while UVA learns a joint video–action latent representation with separate decoding heads (Cheang et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025b). More recent WAMs, including WALL-WM, LingBot-VA, and DreamZero, further couple video generation and action prediction through different unified or causal architectures (Li et al., 2026b;a; Ye et al., 2026). Despite the various WAM architectures, the latents produced from the encoder of VAEs are consumed by the downstream action policy. As a result, it is critical to preserve the latent while quantizing VAEs. This compatibility concern also appears in video-VAE design, where latent-space alignment is used to retain compatibility with a pretrained generator (Zhao et al., 2024; Cai et al., 2026). Our setting is stricter: the downstream action policy is frozen, so the established latent coordinates cannot be adapted after quantization. 

Low-bit quantization for generative models. Existing quantization studies for generative models have primarily focused on the Transformer backbone. Q-Diffusion, SVDQuant, and ViDiT-Q develop low-bit quantization methods for diffusion models, while QVGen introduces QAT for video diffusion models at 4-bit and below (Li et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025a; Huang et al., 2025). In contrast, VAE quantization has received substantially less attention than the Transformer backbone in low-bit diffusion pipelines. PassionSR (Zhu et al., 2025) is among the few works that explicitly quantize the VAE, but it focuses on W8A8/W6A6 PTQ for image super-resolution. Complementary video-VAE work targets compression and decoder efficiency rather than low-bit weight-and-activation execution (Wu et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2026). Although PTQ is time efficient, QAT can achieve higher accuracy, which is critical for downstream action policy. With native NVFP4 support in NVIDIA’s low-precision training stack (NVIDIA, 2025), an effective VAE-specific NVFP4 QAT method is needed for practical low-bit acceleration. 

Interface-aware quantization. Recent work has shown that low-bit quantization can affect intermedi-ate representations and downstream behavior, not only final outputs. Quantized feature distillation and quantization-aware distillation use teacher-guided objectives to mitigate such degradation (Zhu et al., 2023; Xin et al., 2026), while QuantWM, QuantVLA, ActQuant, and DA-PTQ study quantization-induced degradation in world models and vision-language-action policies (Fu et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026a; Akbari et al., 2026; Xu et al., 2026). Analyses of nonuniform floating-point training also identify systematic shrinkage bias in low-bit optimization (Zhao et al., 2026). Studies of latent geometry and frequency structure further show that a reconstruction-preserving latent can still be unfavorable to a downstream generator (Yao et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025a; Skorokhodov et al., 2025; Kouzelis et al., 2025). In contrast, we focus on a different failure mode arising during joint QAT of a pretrained video VAE, where reconstruction optimization persistently shifts the policy-facing encoder representation. We therefore formulate latent preservation as a representation contract during low-bit VAE adaptation.

We study W4A4 quantization under the Blackwell NVFP4 format (NVIDIA, 2025). NVFP4 repre-sents data values in E2M1 and uses two-level scaling consisting of an FP32 per-tensor scale and an E4M3 scale for each contiguous 16-element block. We apply NVFP4 QDQ to the weight and activa-tion operands of convolutional and linear layers. Normalization, nonlinearities, residual additions, and the VAE input and latent boundaries remain in higher precision. During QAT, master parameters and optimizer states remain in FP32, while the selected operands follow the NVFP4 QDQ contract in the forward pass with a straight-through estimator for back-propagation. Direct quantization uses the same arithmetic and layer coverage without parameter adaptation. All low-bit baselines therefore share the same numerical contract, isolating the effect of the optimization strategy. Detailed scaling rules, operand layouts, and mixed-precision scopes are provided in Appendix A.

## 7 CONCLUSION 

Quantizing a WAM VAE is not solely a reconstruction problem: the encoder must also preserve the representation expected by a frozen action policy. We trace joint-QAT failure to decoder activation QDQ, which redirects encoder updates and produces persistent latent drift. LatentQuant prevents further reconstruction-driven encoder drift by aligning the quantized encoder to its high-precision reference, freezing that interface, and then adapting the decoder. Across Wan2.1 and Wan2.2, the resulting NVFP4 VAE retains visual and closed-loop performance near the high-precision systems, while a native Blackwell backend reduces latency, memory, and data movement. Evaluation covers two WAM stacks, LIBERO/RoboTwin, an NVIDIA B300, and convolutional W4A4; broader models, 9hardware, and operators remain open. More broadly, low-bit optimization of shared generative components should preserve the representations consumed downstream, not only reconstructed outputs.

A.1 OPERAND LAYOUT AND MIXED -P RECISION SCOPE 

The logical NVFP4 block layout follows the convolution dataflow. Activations are arranged with channels contiguous at each spatiotemporal position, yielding one E4M3 scale for each 1×16 channel block. Convolution weights are flattened by output channel, with input channels contiguous within each spatial or temporal kernel tap. Incomplete groups are zero padded. The same rule is used for Conv2D, Conv3D, and linear weight and activation operands; zero tensors and underflowed blocks are handled explicitly. The outgoing latent projection belongs to the encoder parameter scope, whereas the incoming latent projection belongs to the decoder scope. The two projections are updated in their respective training stages but remain higher-precision boundaries in the forward path. Raw video inputs, resizing, normalization layers, nonlinearities, residual additions, and the latent interface also remain in higher precision. Throughout the paper, NVFP4 W4A4 therefore denotes this explicit mixed-precision execution contract rather than indiscriminate FP4 execution of every VAE operation. A.2 TRAINING AND DEPLOYMENT STATE 

During QAT, master parameters and optimizer states remain in FP32, while the selected weight and activation operands are passed through strict NVFP4 QDQ in the forward graph. Deployment instead stores supported convolution weights in packed NVFP4 form together with their FP32 tensor scales and E4M3 block scales; activation scaling and packing are performed online. Direct NVFP4, joint QAT, and LatentQuant use the same arithmetic, operand layout, and layer coverage. Differences in reconstruction and downstream behavior therefore arise from parameter adaptation rather than from different quantizers or mixed-precision scopes.

Fixed-observation replay is evaluated separately from interactive real-robot evaluation so that latent and action deviations are not confounded by diverging trajectories. F.4 CORRECTNESS AND FALLBACK POLICY 

Before performance measurement, CUDA QDQ is compared with the reference implementation across operand ranks, channel placements, input dtypes, zero tensors, and E2M1/E4M3 midpoint cases. End-to-end full-sequence and chunked Wan2.2 execution are also compared at 17 frames; 22 measured latent and reconstruction differences remain within the configured numerical tolerances.
