Abstract
We present a perceptual audio coder in which the entropy-coding model and the bit alloca-
tion co-adapt across blocks without model side information. Mantissas are coded by a hy-
brid of bit packing and arithmetic coding with per-band probability models learned online.
Encoder and decoder accumulate identical histograms from decoded blocks and advance
the same model state. The model enters bit allocation directly: per-band rate–distortion
frontiers combine measured perceptual distortion with entropy-coded rate estimates under
the current model, and a dynamic program attains the exact minimum of total objective
perceptual distortion for each block’s budget under the model’s rate estimates, with reser-
voir carry-over. Under a strict per-block rate, entropy-coding-aware bit allocation lowers
this distortion (band noise-to-mask ratio in linear scale) by 15–24 % relative to the same
solver without it, and co-adaptation removes a further 3–4 % (up to 15 % on oboe) that a
backward-adaptive coder alone cannot realize in one pass; a MUSHRA test at 80 kbps per
channel confirms the ranking.
Introduction and Background
Perceptual coders shape quantization noise below the masked threshold of a psy-
choacoustic model and typically allocate a fixed or locally-variable bit budget across
frequency bands [1, 2, 3, 4]. To improve the performance of such coders, we combine
two research lines. The first is allocation: perceptually motivated bit allocation dates
to [1, 5]—Voran’s allocations use a raw bits-times-lines rate model with no entropy
coding—and discrete rate–distortion allocation with general rate models is classical
[6, 7]. Closest to our solver layer, Aggarwal et al. [8] select MPEG Advanced Audio
Coding (AAC) [9] scale factors and Huffman codebooks by a trellis search minimizing
average or maximum noise-to-mask ratio (NMR), exact for each Lagrange multiplier,
with rates taken from the static, standardized Huffman tables. Oger et al. set the
quantizer step from a generalized-Gaussian spectrum model fitted at the encoder and

The second line is adaptive entropy coding: Context-based Adaptive Binary Arith-
metic Coding (CABAC) [11], MPEG Unified Speech and Audio Coding (USAC)’s
context-adaptive arithmetic coder (CAAC) [12], which switches among fixed trained
context tables, and time–frequency context arithmetic coding for G.719 [13] adapt
per symbol from decoded context; in the audio coders among them, the adaptation
state is not used to price allocation candidates. Malvar’s run-length/Golomb–Rice
coder and lapped-transform audio coder likewise adapt from previously decoded data
without side information [14, 15], within the entropy layer only. Conversely, AC-
3’s “hybrid backward/forward adaptive” allocation runs the same core allocation in
encoder and decoder from the decoded spectral envelope [16], but with no entropy
coding in the loop. To our knowledge, no perceptual audio coder closes the loop
between a backward-adaptive entropy model and a discrete rate–distortion-optimal
bit-allocation stage: if the entropy model is learned online from decoded data, the
bit-allocation stage can be made aware of the current coded cost of every candidate
quantization choice—at zero side-information cost.
Our contribution is this closed loop: a decoder-synchronized entropy model is
coupled to entropy-coding-aware perceptual bit allocation, so that the two co-adapt
across blocks without transmitting model parameters.
Concretely: (i) Entropy-
coding-aware allocation. Per-band rate–distortion frontiers are built from mea-
sured quantization distortion (weighted by the masked threshold) and entropy-coded
rate estimates under the current arithmetic model. (ii) Exact and approximate
solvers. A trellis dynamic program (DP) attains the exact minimum of total objec-
tive perceptual distortion for each block’s budget under the model’s rate estimates;
we compare it to a marginal-analysis solver in the style of the generalized Breiman–
Friedman–Olshen–Stone (BFOS) algorithm [7] and a Lagrangian solver [6], exposing
a quality–complexity trade-off. (iii) Causal co-adaptation. Encoder and decoder
advance a shared per-(bit precision, band) histogram model from decoded mantissas;
a control with the coder adapting but the allocation costs frozen isolates the alloca-
tion feedback. (iv) Validation. Objective distortion and CPU time on six standard
excerpts at 64 and 80 kbps/channel, and a listening test in the style of Multiple Stim-
uli with Hidden Reference and Anchor (MUSHRA) [17] at 80 kbps/channel. Under a
strict per-block rate, entropy-coding-aware bit allocation lowers total objective per-
ceptual distortion by 15–24 % relative to the same solver without it, and co-adaptation
removes a further 3–4 % (up to 15 % on oboe) that a backward-adaptive coder alone
cannot realize in one pass; with an unbounded reservoir the increments shrink to 1–
3 % and 0.3–0.5 %, the complete system reaches 57–63 % of conventional water-filling,
and the listening test places the exact-DP conditions 22–25 MUSHRA points above
water-filling, with a further 3.4 points for co-adaptation.
Codec Overview
The coder processes pulse-code-modulated (PCM) audio; in the current implementa-
tion, the two channels of a 16-bit stereo signal sampled at 44.1 kHz are coded inde-


pendently (no joint-stereo tools), and all rates are quoted per channel. Each channel
is analyzed with a sine-windowed modified discrete cosine transform (MDCT) [18] of
N=1024 frequency lines (2048-sample blocks, 50% overlap). Lines are grouped into
K=50 scale-factor bands with equal width on the equivalent-rectangular-bandwidth
(ERB) rate scale [19]; band k holds Lk lines. A block floating-point quantizer with
4-bit scale factors and per-band mantissa precision bk ∈{0} ∪{2, . . . , 16} quan-
tizes each band.
A side-chain fast Fourier transform (FFT)-based psychoacoustic
model (tonal maskers, threshold in quiet [3]) yields per-band signal-to-mask ratios
SMRk; the masking curve rises toward the masker at 27 dB/Bark and falls above
it at 27 −0.367(L−40) dB/Bark, where L is the masker level in dB; the tested im-
plementation leaves L−40 unclamped. Per block and channel, the encoder builds
per-band rate–distortion frontiers, solves the allocation, and writes the bitstream
with per-band arithmetic-coding (AC)/bit-packing selection; both sides then update
the shared entropy model, and unspent bits enter a reservoir.
Hybrid Entropy Coding Layer
Arithmetic coding [20, 21] encodes both scale factors and mantissas. Each AC payload
has an 8-bit header giving its length in bits, which keeps the overhead low, followed
by the coded symbols; payloads longer than 255 bits fall back to bit packing. For
the scale factors of a block and the mantissas of each band, the encoder selects the
shorter of bit packing and arithmetic coding, signaling each choice with one flag bit.
Scale factors are encoded as differences between consecutive bands under a static,
empirically derived probability model; neighboring bands tend to have similar scale
factors, giving the differences lower entropy.
Each (bit precision, frequency band) pair has an initial mantissa probability model:
precision sets the alphabet size, and distributions vary by band. Each model uses a
maximum-likelihood double-geometric fit, with the same geometric distribution for
positive and negative mantissas. This model is updated in the co-adaptive mode,
as described in the Causal Co-Adaptation of Model and Allocation section. Because
lower frequency bands have fewer mantissas per band, plain bit packing tends to beat
arithmetic coding with its 8-bit header there, so bit packing is used for bands 0 to 34
and the hybrid option is enabled from band 35, an empirically chosen threshold.

Marginal analysis (BFOS-style) and Lagrangian relaxation
The marginal-analysis solver, in the style of the generalized BFOS algorithm [7],
starts each band at its cheapest point and repeatedly takes the affordable step of
largest −∆d/∆r; unlike generalized BFOS it does not first take the lower convex hull
of each frontier, and with unequal, indivisible rate steps even convex frontiers do not
guarantee optimality, so it is a heuristic for these discrete frontiers. The Lagrangian
solver [6] minimizes P

B, then greedily projects remaining slack onto the steepest steps. All solvers use the
same frontier construction, so their comparisons isolate the optimization method from
the cost model (in complete runs the reservoirs, and with co-adaptation the models,
evolve separately).

Codec complexity
Frontier construction quantizes and prices every candidate precision before pruning.
Lagrangian search costs O(IK ¯F) for I multiplier evaluations; marginal analysis and
the final greedy fill scan all bands per step, costing O(K2 ¯F). At 80 kbps/channel,
DP takes about 20 times the Lagrangian encode CPU time for about 0.5 % lower
distortion (Table 1). Co-adaptation accumulates histograms each block and rebuilds
coder and rate tables only for changed cells every T blocks; its average encoding
overhead over static AC is about 1 %. The decoder adds histogram accumulation and
occasional table rebuilds.

Experimental Results
Setup
Six excerpts from the European Broadcasting Union (EBU) Sound Quality Assess-
ment Material (SQAM) disc [22] (oboe, glockenspiel, castanets, German male speech,
orchestra, soprano) were selected to evaluate the system at 64 and 80 kbps per chan-
nel. Conditions are labeled solver (entropy mode) as defined in Table 1; all share the
MDCT filterbank, psychoacoustic model, and quantizer, and the AC conditions also
share the bitstream syntax, so only the allocation solver and the entropy model differ.
The initial entropy model was fitted on SQAM material that includes four of the six
excerpts (glockenspiel, speech, castanets, oboe); all conditions share this model, and
we report these four as in-domain and orchestra and soprano, which were not used in
the fit, as out-of-domain. Encode and decode CPU times were measured on a single
core.

Subjective listening test results
A MUSHRA test [17] with a hidden reference and a single 3.5 kHz low-pass anchor
(no 7 kHz mid anchor) compared Water-filling (no AC), Water-filling (static AC),
Exact DP (static AC), and Exact DP (co-adaptive AC) on the six excerpts at 80
kbps/channel; the no-AC stimuli used an earlier configuration without a reservoir or
strategy flags and have about 3 % lower objective distortion than the no-AC condition
in Table 1. All 20 listeners were retained; they used the same Sennheiser HD 600
headphones in a listening room after familiarization with standard coding artifacts,
with the hidden conditions randomized within each trial. Mean scores were 44.9 for
Water-filling (no AC), 55.0 for Water-filling (static AC), 76.6 for Exact DP (static
AC), and 80.0 for Exact DP (co-adaptive AC), compared to 95.1 for the reference
and 27.1 for the anchor (Fig. 2). On paired listener means, co-adaptive minus static
DP is +3.4 points (95 % confidence interval 0.3–6.5, paired t-test p = .035).
Conclusion
We have presented co-adaptive entropy modeling and entropy-coding-aware percep-
tual bit allocation without transmitted model side information; the dynamic program
is exact per block under the model’s rate estimates. Under a strict per-block rate,
entropy-coding-aware bit allocation lowers total objective perceptual distortion by
15–24 % relative to the same solver without it, and co-adaptation removes a further
3–4 % that a backward-adaptive coder alone cannot realize in one pass; both gains
hold in- and out-of-domain. With a reservoir of one block or more, the two increments
shrink to 1–3 % and 0.3–0.5 %, so the co-adaptive gain is realized under every tested
reservoir policy. The Lagrangian solver stays within about 0.5 % of the exact DP at
about 1/20 of its encode CPU time. In the MUSHRA test at 80 kbps/channel with
an unbounded reservoir, both exact-DP conditions score 22–25 points above water-
filling, and co-adaptive DP scores 3.4 points above static DP. Natural extensions are


rate smoothing under a bounded reservoir and a faster exact solver.
