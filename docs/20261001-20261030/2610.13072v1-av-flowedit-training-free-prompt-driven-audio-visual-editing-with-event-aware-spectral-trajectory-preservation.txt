AV-FlowEdit: Training-Free Prompt-Driven Audio-Visual Editing with
Event-Aware Spectral Trajectory Preservation
Shaohua Pan* and Xiao Liu*
Abstract
Joint audio-video diffusion models generate synchronized
content, but editing existing clips requires target changes
without disrupting non-target structure or cross-modal cor-
respondence. We present AV-FlowEdit, a training-free and
inversion-free framework for prompt-driven audio-video edit-
ing. Our key insight is that source–target flow differences
provide both cross-modal editing directions and preserva-
tion cues. Under shared perturbations, they jointly update the
video and audio branches, supporting visual-only, audio-only,
and joint edits while retaining pretrained cross-modal interac-
tions. To suppress drift accumulated during sampling, Cross-
Modal Event-Aware Spectral Trajectory Preservation com-
bines flow saliency, aligned audio activity, multiscale low-
frequency displacement, and diffusion-stage scheduling. This
adaptively preserves unsupported regions while allowing in-
tended audio-visual events. AV-FlowEdit requires no model
training, external masks, or optimization-based inversion. Ex-
periments show strong prompt alignment and temporal con-
sistency, together with competitive audio quality and cross-
modal coherence.
Introduction
Recent advances in video generative modeling have substan-
tially improved spatial fidelity, temporal coherence, and text
alignment (Ho et al. 2022; Lipman et al. 2022; Peebles and
Xie 2023; Kondratyuk et al. 2024). Joint audio-video gen-
erative models further extend this progress by jointly mod-
eling visual content, audio, and text within a unified gener-
ation process, enabling them to capture cross-modal event
semantics and temporal correspondence (Ruan et al. 2023;
Kim et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2024; Ishii et al. 2025).
Editing an observed clip, however, imposes stricter con-
straints than generating a new sample: the model must in-
troduce the requested change while preserving source iden-
tity, background layout, camera motion, and audio-visual
correspondence outside the intended edit (Ku et al. 2024;
Zhang et al. 2025; Mai et al. 2025).
Existing video editing methods can be broadly orga-
nized by whether they require model adaptation and, among
training-free approaches, whether they reconstruct a source
diffusion trajectory. Model-adaptation methods optimize
pretrained generators for a source video or editing task, but

introduce per-instance or task-specific training costs (Wu
et al. 2023; Shin et al. 2024). Training-free methods avoid
parameter updates and instead modify the inference pro-
cess. Many such methods rely on diffusion inversion, first
recovering a source noise or latent trajectory and then re-
sampling it under a target prompt; however, inversion er-
rors can accumulate over time, while strong source-feature
constraints may restrict large changes in content, action, or
scene (Qi et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2023; Ceylan, Huang, and
Mitra 2023; Geyer et al. 2024). More recently, inversion-
free flow-based methods estimate editing directions directly
from differences between source- and target-conditioned ve-
locity fields, avoiding explicit reconstruction of the source
trajectory (Kulikov et al. 2025; Kim et al. 2025). Related
flow-steering principles have also been extended to video
editing, providing a lightweight route to editing video con-
tent, actions, and dynamics without per-example inversion
or training (Li et al. 2025; Kulikov et al. 2026).
Despite these advances, extending inversion-free flow-
based editing to general audio-video editing presents two
unresolved challenges. First, existing methods operate pri-
marily on the visual modality and do not jointly update
the accompanying audio. Pairing them with a separate au-
dio editing system bypasses the bidirectional interactions
learned by a joint audio-video model, potentially weakening
the semantic and temporal correspondence between the two
modalities. Second, iterative source-target flow updates can
accumulate unintended deviations across sampling steps,
leading to background drift, subject deformation, and in-
consistent camera motion. Simple preservation mechanisms
introduce their own trade-offs: fixed source-feature injec-
tion may suppress the requested edit, spatial masks depend
on reliable region-level priors, and uniform low-frequency
constraints may remove the coarse spatiotemporal changes
required by intended actions or synchronized audio-visual
events.
To address both challenges, we propose AV-FlowEdit, a
training-free framework that jointly edits audio and video
using a frozen joint audio-video generative model. At
each sampling step, AV-FlowEdit uses differences between
source- and target-conditioned velocity fields to update both
modalities, thereby retaining their cross-modal interactions
without explicit source inversion. Our key idea is to use
these source–target flow differences not only to drive the de-

Related Work
Video and Audio-Video Foundation Models
Video generation has evolved from spatiotemporal diffusion
models, such as Lumiere (Bar-Tal et al. 2024), to large-scale
DiT-based architectures, including Wan (Wan et al. 2025),
HunyuanVideo (Kong et al. 2024), and LTX-Video (HaCo-
hen et al. 2024). Recent models typically adopt diffusion,
rectified flow, or flow matching objectives, achieving sub-
stantial improvements in visual fidelity and temporal consis-
tency (Ho et al. 2022; Lipman et al. 2022; Esser et al. 2024).
However, these models are primarily designed for content
generation from noise rather than structure-preserving edit-
ing of observed videos.
Most existing systems synthesize video and audio sepa-
rately, making event-level semantic and temporal synchro-
nization difficult(Hua et al. 2025; Ren et al. 2025; Dai et
al. 2026). LTX-2 (HaCohen et al. 2026) instead employs
separate video and audio branches that exchange informa-
tion through cross-attention, enabling joint audio-video gen-
eration while preserving modality-specific representations.
Nevertheless, its generation and retake pipelines are not de-
signed for source-to-target prompt editing, which requires


target-driven changes and source-content preservation to be
balanced within the same inference process.
Training-Based Video Editing
Training-based video editing adapts pretrained generative
models to a source video or a specific editing task. One-
shot methods such as Tune-A-Video and Edit-A-Video op-
timize model parameters using a single source video to pre-
serve its appearance and motion while enabling prompt-
guided modifications (Wu et al. 2023; Shin et al. 2024). Al-
though these methods reduce the need for large paired edit-
ing datasets, they still require source-specific optimization
and may incur additional storage and computational costs
for each input video. Training-free methods instead keep
the pretrained model parameters fixed and control the edit-
ing process through inference-time operations, avoiding per-
video model adaptation.
Training-Free Video Editing
Training-free video editing can be broadly divided into
inversion-based and inversion-free methods.
Inversion-based editing. Early training-free approaches
are largely built upon diffusion inversion, which reconstructs
the latent noise or diffusion trajectory of a source video be-
fore resampling under a target prompt. Representative meth-
ods include FateZero (Qi et al. 2023), Video-P2P (Liu et al.
2023), Pix2Video (Ceylan, Huang, and Mitra 2023), and To-
kenFlow (Geyer et al. 2024). These methods combine source
inversion with attention control, feature injection, or fea-
ture propagation to preserve source appearance and tempo-
ral consistency during editing. However, inversion errors can
accumulate over long video sequences, while strong source
constraints may limit large motion and semantic edits. Ex-
tending inversion to joint audio-video generation is further
complicated by heterogeneous latent spaces and temporal
resolutions across modalities.
Inversion-free editing. Recent methods avoid explicit
inversion by directly modifying the generation trajectory.
FlowEdit (Kulikov et al. 2025) edits videos using the dif-
ference between source- and target-conditioned flow fields,
while FlowAlign (Kim et al. 2025), FlowDirector (Li et
al. 2025), and DynaEdit (Kulikov et al. 2026) further im-
prove trajectory consistency, region-aware control, and dy-
namic content editing. More recently, OmniEdit (Lin, Jin,
and Zhang 2026) extends training-free editing to the audio-
visual domain by jointly enabling lip synchronization and
content editing without model fine-tuning.
Despite these advances, existing methods either focus
on visual editing or target specialized audio-visual scenar-
ios such as talking-head lip synchronization, rather than
general-purpose audio-video semantic editing. Moreover,
their preservation strategies typically rely on fixed attention
guidance or low-frequency constraints, making it difficult
to adaptively balance structural preservation and semantic
editing. Our method follows the inversion-free difference-
flow paradigm, extends it to a unified audio-video trajectory,
and introduces an adaptive spectral trajectory preservation
mechanism for more consistent cross-modal editing.

Evaluation Metrics
To comprehensively evaluate AV-FlowEdit for audio-visual
joint editing, we assess the performance from three perspec-
tives: video editing quality, audio editing quality, and cross-
modal consistency.
Video Quality and Editing Consistency. We adopt
CLIP-F, CLIP-T, Obj., and DINO to evaluate video edit-
ing quality. CLIP-F measures temporal semantic consis-
tency across frames, CLIP-T evaluates the alignment be-
tween edited videos and target prompts, Obj. measures tar-
get object generation accuracy, and DINO evaluates visual
structure preservation.
Audio Quality and Editing Consistency. We use CLAP
and LPAPS to evaluate audio editing quality. CLAP mea-
sures audio-text semantic alignment, while LPAPS evaluates
perceptual consistency between the source and edited audio.
Audio-Visual Consistency. ImageBind similarity (IB.)
and AV-Align are used to evaluate cross-modal consistency,
measuring high-level audio-visual semantic alignment and
temporal synchronization between visual events and audio
events.

Further Analysis
The adaptive trajectory preservation mechanism is designed
to alleviate the conflict between target semantic editing and
source content preservation during the editing process. To
evaluate the contribution of each component, we construct
three variants:
(1) w/o Spectral Preservation: The trajectory preserva-
tion module is removed, and editing is performed only based
on the joint source-target flow difference and trajectory sta-
bilization mechanism.

(2) Fixed Preservation: A fixed-strength structural
preservation constraint is introduced without considering
editing saliency or diffusion-stage information.
(3) Adaptive Preservation (Full): The complete adap-
tive trajectory preservation mechanism is adopted, which
dynamically adjusts the preservation strength according to
editing saliency, multi-scale structural information, and dif-
fusion scheduling.
As shown in Tab. 2, removing the trajectory preserva-
tion mechanism still enables semantic editing, but leads to
degraded structural stability. Introducing fixed preservation
constraints improves content consistency; however, the lack
of adaptive adjustment according to editing regions and dif-
fusion stages may limit the desired semantic transformation.
In contrast, Adaptive Preservation achieves a better balance
between structural stability and editing capability, obtain-
ing improved performance on CLIP-T, Obj., CLAP, and AV-
Align, which validates the effectiveness of the adaptive tra-
jectory preservation mechanism.
Conclusion
In this work, we present AV-FlowEdit, a unified, training-
free, and inversion-free framework for prompt-driven joint
audio-video editing. Experimental results demonstrate that
AV-FlowEdit outperforms existing training-free editing
methods in terms of spatial consistency, temporal coher-
ence, and audio-visual semantic alignment. This work val-
idates the effectiveness of unified audio-video editing based
on joint flow trajectory modeling and provides a promising
