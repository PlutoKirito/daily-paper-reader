Abstract
Pretrained diffusion models provide powerful generative
priors for extreme image compression, but their stochas-
tic, iterative generation processes are not designed to de-
code compressed representations directly. We present Prism-
Codec, which recasts a pretrained Diffusion Transformer
(DiT) as a bitstream-grounded one-step generative decoder.
PrismCodec uses the decoded representation in two comple-
mentary roles: a projected codec state defines the starting
state for generation, while hierarchical visual priors de-
rived from the same decoded latent provide semantic and
structural guidance. To bridge the gap between stochastic,
iterative generation and deterministic one-step decoding, we
develop a progressive adaptation strategy. We first learn
codec-conditioned transport from a noisy neighborhood cen-
tered on the codec state, distill it into a one-step mapping,
and finally anneal the source perturbation to zero during end-

ductions of 70–75% in LPIPS and 75–81% in DISTS over
StableCodec. Code will be released upon acceptance.
1. Introduction
At ultra-low bitrates, learned codecs built on optimized trans-
forms and entropy models [4, 5, 76] can preserve only lim-
ited visual evidence of the source image. Generative com-
pression therefore relies on image generation priors to syn-
thesize details that are not explicitly transmitted. Yet com-
pression differs fundamentally from unconstrained genera-
tion: plausible content is insufficient if it is inconsistent with
the evidence retained in the bitstream. A central challenge is
therefore to exploit powerful generative priors while ground-
arXiv:2610.18607v1  [cs.CV]  13 Oct 2026


ing reconstruction in this limited transmitted evidence.
Pretrained diffusion and flow models offer powerful
visual priors [28, 37, 83, 88], but their generative pro-
cesses are not naturally aligned with compression decoding.
Existing methods expose image-specific compressed evi-
dence to the generative decoder through different interfaces.
OneDC [112] and DiT-IC [93] derive semantic guidance di-
rectly from compressed representations, while AEIC [123]
removes the text-conditioning pathway and reconstructs
from codec latents. NeFIC [17] and StableCodec [122]
retain fixed text conditions but also inject image-specific
information through a decoded anchor and a codec latent,
respectively. Together, these approaches show that effec-
tive generative decoding depends on how transmitted visual
evidence is exposed to the generator. Two questions never-
theless remain: how should that evidence determine both
the generative starting state and the visual guidance, and
how can a pretrained model built for stochastic, iterative
generation be adapted to deterministic one-step decoding?
We introduce PrismCodec, which recasts a pretrained
DiT as a compression decoder whose starting state and visual
guidance are both derived from the bitstream. These two
roles are complementary: the codec-centered state anchors
generation in the transmitted representation, while the visual
priors specify semantic content and spatial structure that
should be preserved during reconstruction. Inspired by re-
cent image-generation studies [31, 94], we combine semantic
and structural visual priors through a shared visual-evidence
pathway. A lightweight decoder renders the decoded codec
features as an internal evidence image, providing a common
image-domain interface through which pretrained visual en-
coders can interpret the same bitstream-derived evidence.
DINOv3 [95] supplies multi-depth dense semantic features,
which are delivered to successive DiT block groups through
depth-matched cross-attention rather than collapsed into a
single condition. This preserves complementary levels of
visual abstraction across the pretrained representation hi-
erarchy. In parallel, the diffusion-native VAE supplies a
spatially aligned structural prior through the latent-input
pathway. Both priors are computed entirely at the decoder
and therefore require no additional conditioning bits.
Visual conditioning alone, however, does not make a
pretrained generator a practical compression decoder. Diffu-
sion and flow models are typically trained to generate from
stochastic sources through iterative transport, whereas prac-
tical compression typically favors deterministic reconstruc-
tion from a transmitted representation under a tight decoding
budget. We therefore progressively reshape the generative
process around the codec state. First, we learn conditional
transport from a stochastic neighborhood centered on the
codec representation to the clean generative latent, so that
the learned trajectory is already grounded in transmitted evi-
dence rather than generic noise. We then distill this codec-
conditioned transport into a direct one-step mapping. Finally,
during end-to-end compression training, we gradually con-
tract the stochastic source distribution onto the deterministic
codec state. This annealing avoids an abrupt shift from the
stochastic source used for flow learning and distillation to
the deterministic input used at deployment, while allowing
the codec and generative decoder to adapt jointly. Fig. 1 sum-
marizes the resulting rate–perception performance. Across
Kodak, CLIC, and DIV2K, PrismCodec achieves LPIPS and
DISTS BD-rate reductions of approximately 70–75% and
75–81%, respectively, over StableCodec. Our main contri-
butions are:
• We recast a pretrained DiT as a bitstream-grounded gen-
erative decoder, using the compressed representation to
determine both its starting state and its visual guidance.
• We introduce hierarchical visual priors that combine multi-
depth dense semantic features with a diffusion-native struc-
tural prior through complementary DiT conditioning inter-
faces.
• We develop a progressive adaptation strategy that moves
from codec-centered stochastic flow learning through
one-step distillation to deterministic end-to-end decoding,
bridging pretrained stochastic generation and practical ex-
treme image compression.
2. Related Work
Generative Image Compression. Generative codecs use
learned priors to recover plausible content at very low bi-
trates.
Adversarial methods improve perceptual quality
through distribution matching [74, 78], while conditional
and residual diffusion reconstruct images from compressed
representations [30, 113]. Recent work increasingly adapts
pretrained generative priors, making bitstream grounding
and efficient decoding central design concerns.
Bitstream Grounding and One-Step Adaptation.
Pretrained-prior codecs expose transmitted evidence through
different conditioning interfaces to guide reconstruction [17,
18, 122]. StableCodec combines a codec latent with struc-
tural correction under fixed text conditioning. OneDC [112]
derives tokenizer-supervised semantic tokens from the hyper-
prior, DiT-IC [93] uses text-aligned compressed features, and
AEIC [123] removes cross-attention. PrismCodec instead
separates bitstream-derived evidence into two roles: a codec
state determines the generative source, while an evidence
image provides hierarchical semantic and diffusion-native
structural guidance through complementary pretrained en-
coders and separate DiT conditioning interfaces.
Fast decoding is addressed through diffusion pretraining
and distillation. CoD [44] pretrains diffusion for compres-
sion, CoD-Lite [43] introduces lightweight one-step decod-
ing, OSCAR [33] supports one-step reconstruction across
multiple bitrates, and FlowCodec [40] adapts a pretrained

3. Method
3.1. Overview
PrismCodec recasts a pretrained DiT as a one-step generative
decoder whose reconstruction is grounded in the received bit-
stream. Given a source image x encoded into a bitstream b,
entropy decoding produces compressed features from which
we derive two complementary forms of information: a codec
state zc that determines the starting state for generation, and
visual priors P that specify the semantic content and spatial
structure to preserve during reconstruction. The decoder
computes
bz0 = Gθ(zc; P),
bx = Dg(bz0),
(1)
where Gθ is a one-step latent mapping implemented by a
DiT, and Dg maps the predicted latent back to image space
through the generative VAE decoder, kept frozen throughout
all stages.
A pretrained diffusion model, however, is designed for
stochastic, iterative generation rather than deterministic de-
coding from a transmitted representation. We therefore adapt
the generative process progressively. Stage I learns condi-
tional transport from a stochastic neighborhood centered
on the codec state, grounding the generative trajectory in
transmitted evidence. Stage II distills this codec-conditioned
transport into a one-step map. Stage III then contracts the
remaining source randomness while jointly optimizing the
codec and generator, continuously aligning the training-time
source with the deterministic codec state used at deployment.
Fig. 2 illustrates the visual interface and Stage-I adaptation,
while Fig. 3 summarizes Stages II and III. Appendix B sum-
marizes the notation.
3.2. Bitstream-Derived Visual Evidence
The decoded bitstream provides the common source for both
generation initialization and visual guidance. Entropy de-
coding and synthesis recover a feature fc, from which Prism-
Codec constructs the codec state zc and an internal evidence
image ex. These two branches expose the same transmitted
evidence through different interfaces: zc determines where
the generative process starts, whereas ex provides an image-
domain view from which pretrained visual representations
can be extracted.

In Stage I, zc centers the stochastic source; after Stage III, it

Shared visual interface. In parallel, a lightweight evidence

it requires no additional transmission. More importantly, it

We lightly supervise ex with an auxiliary objective Lev so that
it retains source layout, appearance, and semantic content.
The objective combines pixel reconstruction with DINOv3
feature similarity. Together, zc and ex expose the same de-


coded representation through the two roles required by the
generative decoder: source initialization and visual guidance.

3.4. Learning a One-Step Generative Decoder
The pretrained generator and the deployed codec differ along
two coupled axes: the former starts from a stochastic source
and follows iterative transport, whereas the latter must start
from a bitstream-determined state and decode in one deter-
ministic step. Rather than imposing both changes at once,
we adapt the generative process progressively. Stage I re-
centers the source on the codec state while retaining stochas-
ticity for flow learning; Stage II distills the resulting codec-
conditioned transport into a one-step map; and Stage III
contracts the remaining source randomness while jointly
adapting the complete compression system to its deployed
deterministic input.

a nominal 768 × 768 resolution, spanning ratios from 1:2 to
2:1. Before Stage I, we pretrain the codec and evidence path
for 20k updates using the evidence MSE and DINO losses

4.1.2. Datasets, Metrics, and Baselines
We evaluate on Kodak [24] (24 images), the CLIC2020 test
set [99] (428 images), and the DIV2K validation set [1] (100
images). We report PSNR and MS-SSIM [104] for distor-
tion, LPIPS [121] and DISTS [21] for perceptual fidelity,
and FID [36] for distributional realism. The four paired met-
rics are computed on full-resolution RGB reconstructions
and averaged over images; FID uses 256 × 256 patches on
CLIC2020 and DIV2K, following Mentzer et al. [74]. We
omit FID on Kodak given its small sample size. We com-
pare with DiffC [102], ResULIC [49], DLF [111], OSCAR
[33], StableCodec [122], OneDC [112], AEIC-ME [123],
DiT-IC [93], and CoD-Lite [43]. We summarize bitrate effi-
ciency with BD-rate [7], using StableCodec as the reference.
Negative BD-rate indicates bitrate savings at equal quality.
4.2. Main Results
4.2.1. Quantitative Evaluation
The main comparison tests whether grounding both the gen-
erative source and visual guidance in the received bitstream
translates into a better rate–perception tradeoff without sys-
tematically sacrificing source fidelity. Fig. 4 reports the
rate–distortion and rate–perception curves in the ultra-low-

4.3. Efficiency Evaluation
We report deployed parameters, encoding and decoding la-
tency, and computation (kMACs/pixel and FLOPs) in Ta-
ble 2. Measurements use synthetic RGB inputs at 2048 ×
2048, batch size one, and an NVIDIA A100 GPU; after
warm-up, latency is averaged over 30 CUDA-synchronized
runs. PrismCodec decodes a 2K image in 2.10 s, compared
with 9.74 s for StableCodec and 3.22 s for OSCAR, while
remaining in the same order of total computation as these
two generative baselines.
4.4. Ablation Studies
We organize the ablations around the three claims underly-
ing PrismCodec: (i) semantic and structural visual priors
provide complementary guidance, with multi-depth seman-
tics contributing beyond a single representation level; (ii)
transmitted evidence should determine the generative source
as well as its conditions; and (iii) explicit one-step distilla-
tion and subsequent source contraction are both important
for adapting stochastic generation to deterministic decoding.
All results report LPIPS- and DISTS-based BD-rate (%) on
DIV2K relative to StableCodec. Each variant trains its own
high-rate model and uses matched fine-tuning schedules to
obtain the five rate points in Sec. 4.1.
Hierarchical visual priors.
Table 3 separates two aspects
of the proposed conditioning design. First, Single semantic
depth repeats the final selected DINOv3 layer across all four
DiT groups, increasing LPIPS- and DISTS-based BD-rate
by 9.90 and 6.27 percentage points relative to the full model.
This isolates the benefit of preserving multiple representation
depths rather than collapsing semantic guidance to one level.
Second, Structural only removes semantic guidance and in-
creases the two BD-rates by 17.87 and 16.32 points, while
Semantic only removes structural input conditioning and in-
creases them by 26.65 and 27.40 points. The degradation
in both single-branch variants supports the complementary
roles of dense semantic guidance and diffusion-native struc-
tural guidance.
Bitstream-grounded source.
With both priors retained,
Zero-centered noise replaces the codec-centered source with
a Gaussian-noise starting state and performs worse than both
the stochastic codec-centered variant (w/o annealing) and
the full model (Table 4). Thus, the gain from zc is not merely
duplicated by the conditioning paths: exposing the bitstream
through the generative source provides an additional and
complementary form of grounding.
Progressive adaptation to deterministic decoding.
Ta-
ble 4 evaluates the two transitions from the Stage-I itera-
tive flow to deterministic one-step decoding. w/o annealing
keeps ρ = 1 through Stage III and inference, using zc +0.3ϵ
as the source, and increases LPIPS- and DISTS-based BD-
rates by 14.52 and 20.23 points. This shows that the stochas-
tic distilled source should be contracted to the determinis-
tic codec state before deployment. w/o distillation skips
Stage II and directly fine-tunes the Stage-I model with the
full model’s annealing schedule, increasing the two BD-rates
by 14.80 and 16.60 points. Together, these results support
both flow-map distillation and source-noise annealing in the
transition to deterministic one-step decoding.
5. Conclusion
We presented PrismCodec, which recasts a pretrained DiT
as a bitstream-grounded one-step decoder for extreme image
compression. The compressed representation determines
both the generative starting state and hierarchical seman-
tic and structural guidance. Through codec-centered flow
adaptation, flow-map distillation, and source-noise anneal-
ing, PrismCodecprogressively transforms stochastic iterative
generation into deterministic one-step decoding. The result-
ing model reconstructs directly from the received bitstream
without additional conditioning bits, yielding an efficient
and effective rate–perception tradeoff.

A. Extended Related Work
This appendix expands the positioning in Sec. 2. We first
place PrismCodec within learned and generative image com-
pression, and then compare the closest pretrained-prior
codecs along the same two questions that motivate our
method: how bitstream-derived evidence enters the gen-
erator and how stochastic generation is adapted to one-step
reconstruction.
A.1. Conventional Learned Compression
Learned codecs have advanced through stronger entropy
models [19, 22, 34, 35, 46, 47, 75, 85], more expressive
transforms [13, 15, 16, 57, 59, 68, 106, 119], and specialized,
scalable, or distilled designs for efficiency [14, 58, 61, 62,
65, 70, 103, 105, 124, 127]. These approaches improve the
coded representation itself; PrismCodec instead focuses on
how a pretrained generative prior should decode the limited
evidence that remains after extreme compression.
A.2. Broader Generative Compression
Generative codecs introduce learned priors to recover visu-
ally plausible content at low rates. Existing families use
adversarial priors [2, 3, 32, 41, 50, 74, 78], discrete rep-
resentations [25, 27, 42, 73, 101, 110], or complementary
representations [66, 71, 111, 126]; related work also studies
bitrate control, progressive decoding, and rate–distortion-
optimized quantization [9, 45, 56, 125].
Diffusion-based codecs reconstruct from compressed
information through conditional denoising [38, 98, 113],
residual generation [30, 64], or pretrained generative priors
[8, 48, 84, 86]. Other methods learn latent guidance and
reconstruction corrections [11, 49, 63, 72, 107, 109], or use
frozen priors for zero-shot coding [26, 80, 100, 102, 108]; re-
cent work further connects diffusion noise with quantization
[23, 87]. CoD pretrains diffusion specifically for compres-
sion [44], and CoD-Lite combines this with a lightweight
one-step convolutional decoder [43]. Accelerated sampling
and distillation [90, 91, 96, 115, 116] also motivate one-step
codecs [33, 82], while FlowCodec uses LoRA-adapted flow
priors for near-terminal one-step latent transport with an
empty prompt [39, 40].
A second line of work concerns the conditioning interface.
Text-guided codecs transmit descriptions or learn language-
aligned representations [10, 51, 52, 54, 55, 81, 97], and
CADC derives text from reconstructions [92]. Image con-
ditions are also widely used for controlled generation and
restoration [12, 77, 118, 120], while REPA aligns diffusion
hidden states with pretrained visual features during training
[117].
For the closest compression methods, Table 5 summa-
rizes how the transmitted representation determines the gen-
erator input and conditions, together with the mechanism
used for one-step adaptation. The categories below concern
the explicit conditioning pathway; all methods also receive
image-specific information from their coded representations.
A.3. Fixed Text Conditions
StableCodec [122] adapts SD-Turbo with a fixed prompt and
a codec-produced latent input. Its auxiliary encoder supplies
source features during compression, and an auxiliary decoder
predicts a structural residual that is added after denoising and
before VAE decoding. The fixed prompt therefore coexists
with substantial image-specific guidance. PrismCodec sepa-
rates the same broad grounding problem into distinct roles:
a codec-centered source determines where generation starts,
while a frozen VAE latent and multi-depth visual tokens
provide structural and semantic guidance through separate
DiT interfaces. StableCodec also tightens the rate penalty
during training; PrismCodec’s source contraction instead
changes the generator’s input distribution as it approaches
deterministic deployment.
NeFIC [17] also retains a fixed prompt, using a decoded
anchor for conditioning and a codec-derived bypass latent as
the input to its video-diffusion generator.
A.4. Learned Semantic Surrogates: OneDC and
DiT-IC
OneDC [112] decodes a semantic token sequence from the
quantized hyperprior and uses it as cross-attention context.
A pretrained generative tokenizer supplies training labels
through an auxiliary prediction task; the tokenizer and pre-
dictor are unnecessary at inference. The resulting hyperprior
tokens retain spatial locality. PrismCodec instead computes
semantic conditions from an evidence image at decoding
time, preserves patch-token sets from multiple pretrained
depths, and assigns them to successive DiT block groups.
This makes the semantic hierarchy an explicit inference-time
condition. OneDC additionally uses diffusion distillation for
perceptual refinement; its tokenizer supervision and refine-
ment therefore address different roles from PrismCodec’s
Stage-II flow-map distillation.
DiT-IC [93] projects compressed features into the text
conditioning space and uses contrastive alignment with text
embeddings during training; inference uses the latent condi-
tion alone. Its adaptation also includes a variance-derived
spatial pseudo-timestep and self-distillation that aligns re-
constructed latents with frozen encoder outputs. PrismCodec
preserves multiple visual representation depths and adds a
separate diffusion-native VAE structural condition. Its one-
step adaptation also follows a different path: Stage I first
learns continuous conditional transport around the codec
state, and Stage II distills the resulting finite-time map. The
source perturbation scale therefore defines a Gaussian neigh-
borhood of the transmitted codec state, whereas DiT-IC’s
pseudo-timestep controls spatially varying reconstruction
strength.

AEIC [123] simplifies the SD-Turbo denoiser by removing
the text encoder, timestep embeddings, and cross-attention

A.6. Auxiliary Reconstruction Guidance: SODEC
SODEC [18] is closely related to our use of a decoded im-
age as a visual interface. Its fidelity decoder reconstructs
an auxiliary image from compressed latents; a frozen ViT
and a learned projection produce a cross-attention condi-
tion. A separate transform supplies the input to an SD2.1
U-Net evaluated at a fixed timestep. Both methods therefore
derive visual guidance and the generative input from the bit-
stream. PrismCodec further separates the conditioning role
into two pretrained representation spaces: semantic features
remain distinct across DINOv3 depths, while a diffusion-
native VAE latent provides spatially aligned structural guid-
ance. The shared evidence image gives both encoders a
common image-domain view of the decoded evidence, and
its auxiliary objective includes paired semantic supervision
(Appendix C.2).
The adaptation mechanisms also differ. SODEC pre-
trains a high-bitrate codec, warms up the diffusion path,
and then tightens the rate penalty during joint optimization.
PrismCodec instead learns codec-centered conditional trans-
port, distills that transport into a one-step map, and then
contracts the stochastic source onto the deterministic codec
state. SODEC’s rate annealing changes the coding con-
straint; PrismCodec’s source-noise annealing changes the
generator’s input distribution.
A.7. Visual Priors and Progressive Flow Adaptation
PrismCodec’s semantic branch uses dense DINOv3 features
[95], while its structural branch uses the frozen VAE encoder.
Both are explicit inference-time conditions derived from
the same bitstream-dependent evidence image. This differs
from representation-alignment methods such as REPA [117],
which use pretrained visual features as training supervision
for diffusion hidden states. More generally, using semantic
features in an auxiliary loss or discriminator is not equivalent
to supplying those features as decoder-side visual context.
The adaptation axis is likewise compositional. Stage I
builds on flow matching and rectified flow [67, 69]; Stage II
uses established AYF-EMD distillation with an interval-
average velocity parameterization [29, 89]. PMRF [79]
provides a related precedent for centering a stochastic flow
source on an informative deterministic estimate. PrismCodec
instead centers the source on an entropy-decoded codec state,
without a posterior-mean assumption or inherited PMRF op-
timality guarantee, and couples that source with hierarchical
visual conditions. Stage III then specializes the distilled
map to the deterministic codec state used at deployment
(Sections 3.4.1–3.4.3).

C.5. Codec-Conditioned AYF-EMD Distillation
We follow the AYF-EMD objective [89] to distill the Stage-I
codec-conditioned flow into a one-step decoder, as sum-
marized in Eq. (10). Teacher and student share the paired
interpolants zt and visual priors P associated with the codec-
centered source z1 = zc + σsϵ. The source-noise scale
remains fixed throughout Stage II; noise annealing starts in
Stage III. We specialize the average-velocity parameteriza-
tion by fixing the destination time to r = 0, so the student
learns transport to the clean endpoint and yields the one-step
map in Eq. (9) at t = 1. The frozen Stage-I DiT serves as the
teacher and provides the student’s initialization. The codec
and all condition-producing modules, including the evidence
decoder, remain frozen; only the student DiT is updated.

C.8. Training and Gradient Paths
Table 8 makes explicit how the progressive adaptation stages
differ in what they optimize. A frozen visual encoder can still
transmit gradients to its input in Stages I and III. The gener-
ative VAE decoder remains frozen throughout, but Stage III
image-loss gradients pass through Dg to the upstream codec
and DiT. Stage II isolates the flow-map distillation problem:
the codec, state projection, evidence decoder, and semantic
projections are frozen, and detaching zc together with the
complete condition P confines map-loss gradients to the
student. No evidence-loss update is applied in this stage.
D. Additional KID Evaluation
The main experiments use FID as a distribution-level com-
plement to paired fidelity metrics. We additionally report
Kernel Inception Distance (KID) [6] on the DIV2K valida-
tion and CLIC2020 test sets to provide a second view of
distributional realism. For PrismCodec, KID is computed
from the same 256 × 256 patches used for FID in Sec. 4.1,
so the comparison changes the distributional metric rather
than the evaluation samples. Fig. 6 reports the measured
rate–KID curves.
E. Limitations and Future Work
PrismCodecprioritizes rate–perception performance and ef-
fective adaptation of a pretrained generative prior, rather
than minimizing model size. As a result, the current decoder
remains relatively large compared with lightweight compres-
sion models. Moreover, high-resolution images are decoded
with overlapping latent tiles to keep memory consumption
manageable. These computational costs accompany the
substantial rate–perception gains obtained by the current
design, but leave clear opportunities for improving deploy-
ment efficiency. Future work will explore more compact
