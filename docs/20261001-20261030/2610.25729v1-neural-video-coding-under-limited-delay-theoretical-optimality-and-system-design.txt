ABSTRACT
Traditional neural video compression (NVC) methods predominantly follow
strictly causal predictive-coding paradigm, treating zero-frame delay as a fixed
constraint rather than delay as an explicit design parameter. From an information-
theoretic perspective, allowing limited frame delay enables a codec to exploit
correlations with future frames within the delay window, thereby reducing un-
certainty that is inaccessible to strictly causal prediction and potentially improv-
ing the rate-distortion tradeoff. In this work, we formalize this intuition through
a rigorous rate–distortion analysis builds on the clique factorization of Markov
networks, and propose Graph-Factored Sequential Coding (GFSC), a principled
framework for sequential video coding under limited delay. To our knowledge,
we provide the first theoretical result showing that, for general K-order Markov
sources with positive distributions, a K-frame delay is sufficient to attain joint-
coding rate-distortion optimality over a nontrivial distortion region, a guarantee
unavailable to zero-delay causal coding in general. In practice, we instantiate
GFSC through lightweight neural modules, allowing existing causal predictive
codecs to be augmented without modifying their core architectures. Experiments
across multiple NVC models and datasets show that GFSC consistently improves
rate-distortion performance over the corresponding causal counterparts. Under
matched delay budgets, GFSC also achieves competitive performance compared
with representative bidirectional codecs.

INTRODUCTION
Video compression can be fundamentally viewed as a sequential lossy source coding prob-
lem (Viswanathan & Berger, 2002; Yang et al., 2011; Ma et al., 2007). Most existing neural video
compression (NVC) models follow a strictly causal predictive coding paradigm (Agustsson et al.,
2020; Lu et al., 2019; 2020; Li et al., 2021; Mentzer et al., 2022), where each frame is encoded
using only previously reconstructed frames and therefore cannot exploit future correlations. For
temporally correlated video, future frames provide predictive information about the current frame
and reduce its uncertainty, potentially improving the rate-distortion tradeoff. In addition, allowing
limited frame delay does not necessarily incur substantial additional latency. At 60 fps, a single-
frame lookahead introduces only 16.67 ms of buffering latency, which is modest relative to the
inference latency of modern NVC models and tolerable in many practical scenarios. Frame delay
should therefore be treated as an explicit design parameter rather than fixed a priori to zero.
The information-theoretic basis of limited delay coding has been studied through delayed sequen-
tial coding (Ma et al., 2007). In particular, for K-order Gauss-Markov sources, a K-frame delay is
sufficient to attain joint-coding rate-distortion optimality over a nonempty distortion region, a guar-
antee that zero-delay causal coding does not generally enjoy. However, it remains unclear whether
this optimality extends beyond Gaussian sources, and the current theory does not provide a con-
structive architectural principle for translating a given delay budget into a practical coding system.
Existing bidirectional codecs (Wu et al., 2018; Djelouah et al., 2019; Xu et al., 2024; Yang et al.,
2020; Yılmaz & Tekalp, 2021) exploit future frames in practice, but typically rely on fixed anchor-
based structures designed for relatively large delay budgets, thereby inefficient in the limited-delay

In this work, we propose Graph Factored Sequential Coding (GFSC), a principled framework for
sequential coding under limited delay. To our knowledge, we are the first to prove that, for general
K-order Markov sources with positive distributions, a K-frame delay is sufficient to attain joint-
coding rate-distortion optimality over a nontrivial distortion region. Our analysis builds on prob-
abilistic graphical models, where K-order Markov dependencies factorize into local clique-wise
interactions. For K-order Gauss-Markov sources, the conditional independence structure further re-
duces to pairwise interactions with linear interpolation sufficient statistics. Figure 1 (left) illustrates
the resulting GFSC architecture under one-frame delay. Although real videos are not assumed to
follow an exact Markov model, their temporal dependencies are strongly local. As shown in Fig-
ure 2, the estimated inverse covariance of video features is approximately banded, with the strongest
dependencies concentrated within a small temporal neighborhood. This empirical locality further
motivates exploiting short, configurable delays.
We show that GFSC can be implemented using lightweight neural modules that exploit delayed-
frame information to reduce the coding uncertainty of the current frame and improve its rate-
distortion tradeoff. Only minimal modifications are required to existing causal NVC architectures.
Experiments across multiple models, datasets, and delay settings show consistent rate-distortion im-
provements over causal baselines and representative B-frame codecs under matched delay budgets.
The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
1. We propose GFSC, a flexible delayed sequential coding framework that uses clique-wise
interactions architecture to systematically exploit a configurable frame-delay budget.
2. We establish rigorous rate-distortion guarantees for GFSC. For general K-order Markov
sources, we prove that K-delay GFSC attains joint-coding rate-distortion optimality over a
nontrivial distortion region. For K-order Gauss-Markov sources, the clique-wise construc-
tion further reduces to pairwise linear interpolation.
3. We realize GFSC using lightweight neural modules that can be integrated into existing
causal NVC models with minimal architectural changes. Experiments demonstrate consis-
tent rate-distortion gains over causal baselines and competitive performance against repre-
sentative B-frame codecs under matched delay budgets.

A key result in Ma et al. (2007) shows that, for K-order Gauss-Markov sources, a K-frame delay
is sufficient for DSC to asymptotically attain the joint-coding rate-distortion function in the high-
resolution regime. However, this leaves two gaps. First, the result is established for the Gauss-
Markov setting and does not characterize whether the same optimality extends to general K-order
Markov sources. Second, DSC specifies the admissible information flow and its fundamental limits
and does not provide an architectural principle for realizing these limits in practical coding systems.
In this work, we address both issues. We first establish the joint-coding optimality of K-delay
sequential coding for a broader class of K-order Markov sources through a probabilistic graphical
model analysis. We then introduce GFSC as a constructive realization of this structure.

JOINT-CODING OPTIMALITY FOR MARKOV SOURCES
3.4.1
GENERAL MARKOV SOURCES
As discussed in Section 3.1, a K-order Markov source factorizes over consecutive cliques Cj =
{j, . . . , j + K}. This graphical structure motivates a clique-wise auxiliary construction: each aux-

of overlapping auxiliaries captures the global source dependence through local interactions. For the
optimality proof, we construct a particular class of such auxiliaries satisfying stronger Markov con-
ditions than those required by GFSC. These conditions preserve the relevant dependency structure
while remaining admissible under the GFSC-K information-flow constraints.

EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS
We conduct experiments on several NVC models based on causal predictive coding, including
the DCVC (Li et al., 2021), video compression transformer (VCT) (Mentzer et al., 2022), and
DHVC (Lu et al., 2024). We compare the rate-distortion performance of the GFSC extension and
its causal counterparts. We further compare against both neural and conventional delayed coding
baselines (bidirectional) under the same delay constraints. For quantitative comparison, we report
BD-rate results on UVG (Mercat et al., 2020) and MCL-JCV (Wang et al., 2016).

naturally handles boundary cases with fewer than K available future frames. Architectural details

RELATED WORK
Neural video compression. Recent NVC methods have achieved rate-distortion performance com-
petitive with conventional video codecs. Most existing approaches follow a strictly causal predictive
coding paradigm, in which each frame is encoded using only previously reconstructed frames. Early
methods adopt motion compensation followed by residual coding (Lu et al., 2019; Agustsson et al.,
2020; Lin et al., 2020), whereas more recent approaches directly model temporal dependencies in
the latent space through learned conditional coding (Li et al., 2021; Sheng et al., 2022; Li et al.,
2023; 2024; Mentzer et al., 2022). Although these methods support zero-frame delay coding, they
cannot exploit correlations with future frames. Bidirectional codecs address this limitation through
interpolation-based prediction (Wu et al., 2018; Djelouah et al., 2019; Xu et al., 2024) or bidirec-
tional conditional modeling (Yang et al., 2020; Yılmaz & Tekalp, 2021; Sheng et al., 2025). How-
ever, they typically rely on fixed anchor-frame structures and are designed for relatively large frame

CONCLUSION
We introduced GFSC, a general framework that treats frame delay as an explicit coding resource
rather than fixing sequential video coding to the causal setting. By exploiting the clique structure
of K-order Markov sources, we established the first rigorous result showing that a K-frame delay
can attain joint-coding rate-distortion optimality for a broad class of general Markov sources. For
Gauss-Markov sources, this construction further reduces to pairwise interactions with linear inter-
polation sufficient statistics, revealing a simple structure behind the optimal auxiliaries. GFSC can
be implemented with lightweight neural modules and integrated into existing NVC models with
minimal architectural changes. Experiments consistently demonstrate improved rate-distortion per-
formance over causal coding and bidirectional codecs under matched delay budgets. Together, these
results identify limited delay as a fundamental and practically useful design dimension for neural
video compression, bridging information-theoretic optimality and implementable coding architec-
tures. Limitations and future directions are discussed in Appendix E.

C-NC (causal encoder, noncausal decoder). In the C-NC setting, the encoder operates causally,
while the decoder is allowed a delay of K frames. The encoding and decoding mappings are given

NC-NC (noncausal encoder, noncausal decoder). In NC-NC setting, both encoders and decoders

Xℓ.

each frame with an additional delay of K2.
These three setups characterize different ways of distributing the delay budget between the encoder
and decoder, and together provide a unified framework for analyzing delayed sequential coding
systems. We illustrate the corresponding system architectures for the three delayed setup in Figure 8.
From a theoretical perspective, Corollary 6.1 in Ma et al. (2007) shows that all three setups achieve
the same sum-rate performance for a given total delay K.

SUMMARY
We provide a simple two-frame Gaussian example to illustrate the behavior of the proposed ISC
framework. Consider jointly Gaussian sources (X1, X2) with a quadratic distortion measure.
Symmetric distortion.
We first consider the symmetric setting D1 = D2 = D. Figure 12 (left)
shows the rate-distortion curves for joint coding, causal sequential coding, and ISC-1. In this case,
the sum-rate is denoted by Rsum(D2). As shown, ISC-1 achieves the same rate-distortion per-
formance as joint coding in the high-resolution regime by introducing auxiliary variables through
interpolation. Moreover, it strictly outperforms causal coding across the entire rate region.
Asymmetric distortion.
We next consider the case where D1 is fixed and vary D2. Figure 12
(right) shows the corresponding rate-distortion curves, denoted by RD1,sum(D2). In this setting, the
interpolation coefficients can be optimized according to the distortion constraint, and ISC achieves
joint coding optimality across the entire feasible rate region.
Discussion.
This example illustrates how interpolation-based auxiliary variables enable ISC to
capture delayed dependencies efficiently. While the exact optimality result is specific to Gaussian
44


sources, it provides a clear design principle for practical video coding: temporal dependencies can
be exploited through local interpolation within a limited delay window without sacrificing coding
efficiency.
D
EXPERIMENT DETAILS
The proposed GFSC architecture is model-agnostic and can be integrated into different neural video
compression (NVC) models. In this section, we provide more implementation details about model-
training, evaluation, and baseline setups. We also explicitly show how GFSC can be incorporated
into DCVC (Li et al., 2021) architecture, which serves as our primary baseline. The same integration
principle can be applied to other NVC architectures, such as VCT and DHVC, with only minor
modifications.
D.1
TRAINING SETUP AND OVERVIEW
Training and evaluation. All models are trained on Vimeo-90K (Xue et al., 2019) using 7-frame
sequences (GOP = 7), with the remaining training hyperparameters following the corresponding
original implementations. In our implementation, we focus on evaluating the effectiveness of the
proposed GFSC mechanism rather than optimizing for peak performance. Therefore, for VCT and
DHVC, we adopt a simplified training procedure corresponding to the final stage of VCT, where all
components (encoder, decoder, and entropy model) are trained jointly from scratch. Specifically, we
train the models for 50 epochs using their original rate-distortion objectives and optimization set-
tings. For DCVC, we adopt the full multi-stage training procedure of the original model as in Zhang
& Zhu (2025) and introduce the delayed-frame modules only in the final training stage. For evalua-
tion, we report results on UVG (Mercat et al., 2020) and MCL-JCV (Wang et al., 2016). Following
standard evaluation protocols, we encode the first 96 frames of each sequence with GOP = 32. We

Rate-distortion training.
We follow the standard rate-distortion optimization used in DCVC Li
et al. (2021). First, the rate-distortion loss function is defined as
L = R + λD,
where R denotes the bitrate and D denotes the distortion. Both are averaged across the video
sequence batch during training. The trade-off hyperparameter λ is selected from λ ∈0.01 ×
{2, 1, 2−1, 2−2, 2−3, 2−4}, covering both low- and high-rate regimes. For other settings, we use
the Adam optimizer with a batch size of 8, an initial learning rate of 1 × 10−5 and aux learning rate
of 1 × 10−3. Training is performed for 30 epochs with a linear learning rate schedule: the learning
rate is warmed up for the first 2k steps and then linearly decayed to 1×10−6. All models are trained
on a single NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU using 2 days.

Consider an L-frame video sequence. In RA coding, the encoder first buffers a short frame group
and performs bidirectional prediction within the group. For example, in an I-B-B-B-I structure,
intermediate B-frames depend on both past and future anchor frames. As a result, the encoder must
wait for the arrival of the future I-frame before encoding the intermediate frames, leading to an
effective practical delay determined by the distance between the first B-frame and the future anchor
frame. The decoder then reconstructs frames in coding order and reorders them back to display
order. Figure 13 illustrates the corresponding delay configurations.
We consider two representative delayed coding baselines: DCVC-B Sheng et al. (2025) and HM-
RA. For DCVC-B, the neural architecture allows flexible extension to delay settings. Therefore,
both K = 1 and K = 2 are implemented by customizing the reference frame lists for each frame.
For HM-RA, the hierarchical GOP structure is designed primarily for even GOP sizes. Accordingly,
we modify the standard encoder_randomaccess_main.cfg to implement 1-delay and 3-
delay settings using I-B-I and I-B-B-B-I structures, respectively. The intermediate I-B-B-I
configuration is not directly supported under the standard HM-RA hierarchical coding structure.

contains separate I-frame and P-frame models. For P-frame coding, we distinguish two branches:

modules between the analysis transforms and the subsequent coding modules, while leaving the

D.3
ADDITIONAL EXPERIMENTAL ANALYSIS
We further evaluate GFSC-enhanced DCVC on the 4K LIVE-HDR dataset (Shang et al., 2022).
Compared with causal DCVC, GFSC achieves BD-rate reductions of 3.78%, 4.50%, and 5.11%
for delays K = 1, 2, 3, respectively. These results show that exploiting information from delayed
frames also improves rate-distortion performance for 4K video.
E
LIMITATIONS
Despite the theoretical and empirical results established in this work, several questions remain open.
First, our joint-coding optimality result is characterized over a nontrivial distortion region. Extend-
ing this characterization beyond the current region, and determining whether alternative auxiliary-
variable constructions can enlarge the optimality regime, are important directions for future work.
This limitation concerns only the extent of the characterized region and does not affect the estab-
lished optimality guarantee within it.
Second, while the general Markov analysis establishes the existence and graphical structure of JC-
optimal clique-wise auxiliary variables, it does not yield a simple closed-form parameterization for
arbitrary source distributions. For Gauss-Markov sources, the additional Gaussian structure makes
this construction explicit and reduces the clique-wise interactions to pairwise linear interpolation.
Developing similarly tractable constructions for broader non-Gaussian source families is an inter-
esting theoretical direction.
Finally, the computational cost of GFSC grows approximately with the delay budget because addi-
tional future-frame interactions must be modeled. This overhead is moderate in the limited-delay
regime studied in this work, but scaling GFSC to substantially longer delays while controlling com-
putation and memory remains an important engineering direction.
