Abstract
Regression-based RL for flow models mandates sampling
without classifier-free guidance (CFG) to align the rectifi-
cation target with the reference marginal velocity. Extend-
ing Advantage-Weighted Rectification (AWR), which dynam-
ically scales this regression target based on reward, to video
generation encounters a cold-start pathology: unguided roll-
outs collapse into low-fidelity regions where intra-group re-
ward gaps remain wide but no longer track perceptual quality,
rendering the optimization target arbitrary. Conversely, utiliz-
ing guided rollouts to restore fidelity, while keeping the regres-
sion target tied to the guided sampling distribution, provably
triggers a geometric guidance blow-up under exponential-
moving-average (EMA) updates. We propose VideoAWR,
which resolves this tension by untying sampling from the op-
timization anchor. An EMA reference policy generates guided
rollouts to restore reward separability, while the trainable pol-
icy is rectified against the unguided conditional anchor. We

Introduction
Reinforcement learning for generative alignment (Black et al.
2024; Fan et al. 2023; Rafailov et al. 2023) extends to text-
to-video (Prabhudesai et al. 2024; Liu et al. 2024; Xing
et al. 2024) primarily through policy-gradient (Shao et al.
2024; Liu et al. 2026a; Xue et al. 2025) and regression-
based methods (Zheng et al. 2025; Fu et al. 2026). The latter
arXiv:2610.13338v1  [cs.CV]  9 Oct 2026


embeds the reward directly into the velocity-matching ob-
jective (Peters, Mulling, and Altun 2010; Peng et al. 2019),
bypassing intractable trajectory-likelihood estimation over
long rollouts. However, regression dictates strict data collec-
tion constraints: the target anchor—the marginal velocity of
a reference path (Lipman et al. 2022; Liu, Gong, and Liu
2023)—doubles as the sampling field. Because CFG (Ho
and Salimans 2022; Dhariwal and Nichol 2021) distorts this
marginal, rollouts must theoretically remain unguided.
In video generation, this unguided requirement de-
grades optimization. Sampled without CFG, models like
Wan2.1 (Wan et al. 2025) yield videos confined to a low-
fidelity region where reward models (Wu et al. 2023; Ma
et al. 2025; Liu et al. 2026b) lose discriminative capacity
(Figure 1). The induced intra-group ranking becomes arbi-
trary, causing initial reward degradation. Conversely, restor-
ing fidelity via guided rollouts (Guo et al. 2025; Frans et al.
2025) while anchoring on that same CFG field induces
inter-round instability. We prove that under EMA updates,
this coupling geometrically compounds the conditional–
unconditional gap. Must guided exploration then be paid
for with unstable training?
We propose VideoAWR, which explicitly separates the
sampling distribution from the optimization anchor. The
EMA reference policy generates guided rollouts to maintain
fidelity and reward separability, while the trainable policy
is rectified against the unguided conditional anchor. Charac-
terizing the population optimum of this decoupled objective
demonstrates that the mismatch is mathematically exact: the
centered advantage cancels the anchor in expectation. This
isolates the unguided conditional velocity and the reward-
correlated covariance increment, strictly filtering out the
guidance offset. Consequently, inference operates via a sin-
gle forward branch, and the conditional–unconditional gap
shifts only by an advantage-bounded increment, preserving
inference-time CFG semantics. This structurally contrasts
with guidance distillation (Meng et al. 2023; Tang et al.
2025), which forfeits inference-time guidance by baking the
field directly into the network.
Furthermore, the point-wise evaluation of the regression
objective decouples the training timestep from the rollout
schedule, bypassing limitations of single-step schemes that
tie timesteps to the sampling discretization or share them
across groups (Li et al. 2025; He et al. 2026, 2025). We
introduce Decoupled Timestep Rectification, assigning an
independent timestep per rollout without bias. The exact im-
portance correction collapses to a scalar naturally absorbed
by group normalization, a structural flexibility unique to re-
gression objectives. Our contributions are as follows:
• Guided rollout with a stable anchor. We identify the
cold-start pathology of unguided video flow RL and prove
that its conventional remedy—anchoring the target on
the guided CFG field—diverges. Under EMA updates,
the gap between the conditional and unconditional fields
compounds geometrically.
• Anchor invariance of the behavior-sampled optimum.
We resolve this divergence by separating the guided sam-
pling distribution from the unguided conditional anchor.
We prove its population optimum is exactly the unguided
field plus the reward-driven increment; the centered ad-
vantage strictly filters out the guidance offset in expec-
tation. This inherently enables CFG-free inference while
preserving inference-time guidance semantics.
• Decoupled Timestep Rectification. We introduce an un-
biased per-rollout timestep estimator that improves sam-
ple efficiency by allowing a single prompt group to super-
vise multiple timesteps. We show its residual importance
correction collapses to a scalar natively absorbed by group
normalization.
• Empirical validation under matched compute. Evalu-
ated under equal GPU-hours on Wan2.1-1.3B and 14B,
VideoAWR achieves higher visual and motion rewards
than single-step baselines, optimizes stably throughout,
and retains its gains under both CFG-free and CFG-
enabled inference.

Related Work
Alignment for Video Generation.
Following the success
of RLHF in language and image domains (Ouyang et al.
2022; Black et al. 2024; Fan et al. 2023), aligning text-to-
video models with human preferences has drawn increas-
ing attention. Direct preference optimization has been ex-
tended to video by constructing preference pairs at differ-
ent granularities (Liu et al. 2024; Wu et al. 2026; Zhang
et al. 2024). Parallelly, online RL formulations treat video
diffusion and flow processes as multi-step Markov decision
processes, optimizing reverse-process transitions via policy
gradients (Black et al. 2024; Fan et al. 2023). For continu-
ous flow-based video models, Flow-GRPO (Liu et al. 2026a)
and DanceGRPO (Xue et al. 2025) introduce stochasticity
into ODE integration to yield tractable transition probabili-
ties and enable group-relative credit assignment. To mitigate
the heavy computational overhead of long spatiotemporal
trajectories, recent extensions adopt mixed ODE–SDE sam-
pling or single-timestep trajectory supervision (Li et al. 2025;
He et al. 2026). However, these policy-gradient derivatives
struggle when the unguided generation quality is severely
degraded at cold start.

Classifier-free guidance boosts conditional alignment by
extrapolating conditional velocity fields away from uncon-
ditional ones, at the expense of an additional evaluation
branch (Ho and Salimans 2022); guidance distillation avoids
this overhead by regressing the combined field into a single
network (Meng et al. 2023). For flow-based preference
alignment, DiffusionNFT (Zheng et al. 2025) models posi-
tive and negative transitions on the forward process, while
FlowAWR (Fu et al. 2026) anchors advantage-weighted
rectification on the reference policy’s velocity field. These
methods work for image generation without CFG at sam-
pling time, but in video they meet the cold-start pathology
above. VideoAWR instead confines CFG to the sampler.

4
Method
VideoAWR adapts the advantage-weighted rectification
paradigm to the alignment of text-to-video flow models,
which presents two primary challenges. First, the reference
field is structurally mismatched to the reward signal: drop-
ping CFG destroys the reward separability that relative ad-
vantage estimation depends on, yet anchoring the target on
the amplified CFG field makes training diverge (Sec. 4.1).
Second, the training timestep must be sampled cheaply and
without bias, ideally decoupling the temporal supervision
from the terminal reward, allowing optimization via point-
wise regression to maximize sample efficiency (Sec. 4.3).

5
Experiments
5.1
Experimental Setup
Datasets and Models. Following DanceGRPO (Xue et al.
2025), we use their prompt set for training and the same
split of 300 prompts in Flash-GRPO (He et al. 2026) for
evaluation. We build on the Wan2.1 family (Wan et al. 2025),
validating VideoAWR on both the 1.3B and the 14B variants.
Implementation Details. Guided rollouts use a CFG scale
of 4.5, 20 sampling steps and group size G = 8 for both
backbones, with an EMA reference policy. Full training and
evaluation details are in Appendix B.
Baselines. We compare against Flow-GRPO (Liu et al.
2026a), Flow-GRPO-Fast, Flash-GRPO (He et al. 2026), and
DiffusionNFT (Zheng et al. 2025). Flow-GRPO uses its of-
ficial video-RL configuration, which trains only on the first
half of the denoising timesteps. As Flow-GRPO-Fast’s few-
step training is conceptually aligned with MixGRPO (Li et al.
2025), we evaluate it with single-step updates (Flow-GRPO-
Fast1); Flash-GRPO likewise supervises one timestep per
update but shares it across the prompt group.
Rewards and Evaluation. We optimize two complemen-
tary rewards. For visual quality we use HPSv3 (Ma et al.

Ablation Study
We ablate the three design choices of VideoAWR on Wan2.1-
1.3B with the HPSv3 reward.
Guided Rollout. We compare our design against two alterna-
tives with the identical objective but different sampling and
anchoring: (i) no-CFG sampling, rolling out without guid-
ance; and (ii) anchor-on-CFG, rolling out with guidance but

ure 5(a), no-CFG sampling falls behind during cold start and
does not close the gap after recovering (6.1 vs. 7.1 at 600
GPU hours), while anchor-on-CFG decreases from the first
checkpoint and diverges before 300 GPU hours, as predicted
by Proposition 1.
Decoupled Timestep. We compare independent timesteps
(DTR) against a group-shared timestep (Flash), each under
our behavior-anchored objective and under a GRPO objec-
tive. In Figure 5(b) both of our variants exceed both GRPO

under our objective (Ours 7.1 vs. Ours-Flash 6.8) and hurts
under GRPO (DTR-GRPO 2.3 vs. Flash-GRPO 4.2). This
follows from Sec. 4.3: a policy-gradient objective weights
each update by an importance ratio evaluated along the sam-
pled trajectory, so its timestep must stay tied.

non-monotone: G = 4 tracks G = 8 early but saturates near

is more accurate per update but completes too few updates

6
Conclusion
VideoAWR formulates “guided rollout, unguided training”
as behavior-anchored advantage-weighted rectification. By
restricting guidance strictly to the sampling phase, the op-
timized policy isolates the reward-correlated velocity in-
crement and filters out the guidance offset, thereby pre-
venting the geometric divergence associated with CFG-
anchored training. Consequently, the fine-tuned model sup-
ports both inference regimes: it enables efficient CFG-free
single-branch generation while preserving the original se-
mantics of inference-time CFG. Integrated with Decoupled
Timestep Rectification, VideoAWR provides a stable online
optimization framework that maintains better inference flex-
ibility for both CFG-free and CFG-enabled inference.
Limitations and future work.
Proposition 2 mathemat-
ically permits arbitrary behavior distributions, establishing
that the optimization anchor is invariant to the sampling pol-


icy. Currently, VideoAWR solely utilizes CFG-guided roll-
outs from the EMA backbone. Consequently, optimization
is bounded by the base model’s inherent generation capa-
bilities; prompts that the backbone fundamentally fails to
render cannot yield informative reward gradients, regardless
of the guidance scale. Because anchor invariance supports
any behavior distribution, integrating off-policy or out-of-
distribution terminals requires no modification to the under-
lying objective. Investigating this off-policy data integration
constitutes a primary direction for future work.

Backbones and Data.
We fine-tune the official Dif-
fusers checkpoints of Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B and Wan2.1-T2V-
14B (Wan et al. 2025). All videos are generated at 480×832
resolution with 81 frames at 16 FPS. The text encoder pro-
cesses a maximum of 512 tokens. Training is conducted in
bfloat16 mixed precision.
Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning.
We employ rank-32
LoRA adapters (α = 64, Gaussian initialization) on the
query, key, value, and output projections of both the stan-
dard and newly introduced attention blocks, while keeping
the base network frozen.
Rollout and Sampling.
Guided rollouts are generated us-
ing the frozen behavior policy adapter. We use the native
flow-matching scheduler with 20 integration steps and a CFG
scale of s = 4.5. The primary configuration uses a prompt
group size of G = 8. A distributed repeat sampler ensures all
G rollouts for a single prompt are constructed synchronously.
For Decoupled Timestep Rectification (DTR), we align with
Flash-GRPO (He et al. 2026) and restrict single-step super-
vision to the first 51% of the rollout schedule (i.e., candidate
indices {0, . . . , 9} for a 20-step schedule). For each sample,
the training timestep index is drawn uniformly and indepen-
dently.

Video quality is evaluated using
the HPSv3 reward (Ma et al. 2025). We score individual
frames and average the top 30% to mitigate spurious vari-

Training is executed on a

Policy Synchronization and Evaluation.
The behavior
adapter (θold) is synchronized from the trainable policy via
a scheduled EMA update: exact copies for the first 75 steps,
a linear transition up to step 208, and an EMA decay of
λ = 0.999 thereafter. Separately, an evaluation EMA with
a 0.9 decay is maintained over the trainable parameters for
checkpointing and inference.
C
Validation of CFG-Free and CFG-Enabled
Inference
Why a sampling-distribution anchor can impair CFG.
Anchoring a regression target directly on the guided sampling
field provides a straightforward route to CFG-free inference:
the conditional branch internalizes the amplified behavior
field and can therefore generate strong samples without eval-
uating an unconditional branch. However, this changes the
semantics of the conditional–unconditional gap. Applying
CFG again may extrapolate from a conditional field that has
already absorbed guidance, effectively amplifying the same
offset twice.
We first examine this effect using the official Diffusion-
NFT checkpoint (Zheng et al. 2025). As shown in Table 2, the
released model is strong under CFG-free inference, but en-
abling CFG decreases every reported Task and DrawBench
metric. The largest drops occur on OCR (0.90 →0.69),
HPSv2.1 (0.330 →0.273), ImageReward (1.48 →1.03),
and the two PickScore evaluations (23.36 →20.99 and
23.79 →22.03). This consistent degradation is compatible
with guidance having been absorbed into the learned condi-
tional field, although the comparison alone does not identify
the mechanism causally.
This observation motivates the dual-mode requirement


targeted by VideoAWR: guided behavior should improve
exploration without being baked into the conditional an-
chor. The resulting policy should retain the learned reward-
driven improvement under CFG-free inference while pre-
serving a meaningful conditional–unconditional gap for op-
tional inference-time CFG.
Video flow alignment.
We next evaluate the same dual-
mode requirement on Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B optimized with
HPSv3. Figure 6 isolates the behavior sampling distribution
while holding the optimization anchor fixed to the unguided
conditional field vold in both settings. Panel (a) uses no-CFG
behavior rollouts. Although inference-time CFG provides a
large initial advantage, this benefit gradually vanishes dur-
ing training. After convergence, applying CFG no longer
improves the policy and instead yields a slightly lower eval-
uation reward than CFG-free inference.
Panel (b) uses guided behavior rollouts while retaining the
same unguided conditional anchor. Both inference modes
improve faster and reach higher rewards than their counter-
parts in panel (a). Moreover, the CFG-enabled curve remains
above the CFG-free curve as training converges. Thus, guided
behavior improves the single-branch policy without requir-
ing the guided field to enter the regression anchor, while
preserving the benefit of optional inference-time CFG after
training.

More Qualitative Evaluation
We qualitatively compare vanilla Wan2.1 with its Flash-
GRPO and VideoAWR aligned variants at the 14B model
scales. As shown in Figure 7, VideoAWR consistently gen-
erates videos with higher visual fidelity, richer scene details,
and smoother motion dynamics.
As shown in Figure 7, VideoAWR shows consistent im-
provements across diverse scenes. In the animated scenes,
the little girl riding a bicycle and Bigfoot walking through
the snow exhibit more plausible motion dynamics and higher
aesthetic quality. The scenes depicting a middle-aged driver
speeding along a rural road in a school bus and a blue-eyed
man decelerating a black sports car demonstrate greater se-
mantic consistency and more coherent motion. The scene
featuring a green-furred panda playing a guitar beside a
campfire against a snow-covered mountain backdrop presents
richer visual details and an enhanced depth-of-field effect. Fi-
nally, the scene depicting a modern art museum displaying
abstract paintings exhibits higher visual fidelity and a better
organized spatial layout.
These results confirm that VideoAWR effectively im-
proves visual aesthetics, temporal coherence, and prompt
adherence across different model scales and content types.
