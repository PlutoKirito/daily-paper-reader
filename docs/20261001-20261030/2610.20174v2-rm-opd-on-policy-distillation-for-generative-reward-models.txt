ABSTRACT
Reinforcement Learning on Mixed-domain preference data (Mix-RL) has become
the mainstream approach for training general-purpose Generative Reward Mod-
els (GRMs). However, Mix-RL suffers from two critical bottlenecks:the reward
sparsity induced by outcome rewards, and the cross-domain interference arising
from jointly optimizing heterogeneous capabilities, which together give rise to
imbalanced capability acquisition and pervasive reward hacking. Inspired by the
success of On-Policy Distillation (OPD) in multi-domain capability integration, we
introduce RM-OPD (Reward Modeling via On-Policy Distillation) , a two-stage
framework that separates specialist acquisition from capability integration. First,
we cultivate domain-specialized teacher models via single-domain GRPO fine-
tuning, allowing each expert to reach its performance ceiling in isolation; Then we
consolidate these teachers into one student model through (i) routed on-policy dis-
tillation, which provides dense signed token-level supervision on student-generated
critiques; (ii) domain contribution reweighting, which reweights token guidance
toward target domain shares, preventing specific domains from dominating opti-
mization; and (iii) optional criteria context distillation, which conditions teachers
on domain-specific evaluation criteria to sharpen token-level supervision. Across
multiple RM benchmarks and two base models, RM-OPD consistently outperforms
vanilla Mix-RL and other strong baselines, achieving overall superior performance
and exhibiting an emergent ”teacher-surpassing” effect. These results establish
RM-OPD as a promising paradigm for building generalist GRMs.

INTRODUCTION
Generative reward models (GRMs) have emerged as a powerful approach to enhance the general
capabilities of large language models (LLMs) by providing robust and more reliable reward sig-
nals (Zhang et al., 2025a; Mahan et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2026). Unlike traditional scalar reward
models that compress evaluation into a numerical score, GRMs generate natural-language rationales
with detailed critiques before issuing a final judgment, leading to significantly higher robustness,
interpretability, and generalizability through explicit reasoning (Ankner et al., 2024; Zhang et al.,
2025a; Yu et al., 2025b). These advantages become particularly important in general-domain reward
modeling, as the evaluation criteria for rewards are more diverse and complex, and explicit references
or ground-truth are often unavailable (Liu et al., 2025b). Generalist reward modeling is thus crucial
for improving the performance of LLMs across broader applications. However, building such a
generalist GRM requires distinct evaluation capabilities demanded by different domains: mathematics
requires step-level logical verification, code requires reasoning about functional correctness and edge
cases, and safety requires intent-sensitive judgment and calibrated refusal (Lambert et al., 2025; Liu
et al., 2025a; Zhou et al., 2025). The central challenge is therefore to consolidate these heterogeneous
evaluation capabilities into a single GRM while enabling them to coexist without mutual degradation.
A prevailing solution for building generalist GRMs is reinforcement learning on mixed multi-domain
data (Mix-RL), typically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024; Chen
et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2025b), which pools prompts from various domains and optimizes one shared

Recent successes of on-policy distillation (OPD) in multi-domain LLM post-training suggest a
different paradigm. OPD consolidates heterogeneous capabilities from independently trained spe-
cialists into one student model through dense token-level supervision on the student’s own rollouts,
which is widely adopted in frontier models (Agarwal et al., 2024; Gu et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2026;
DeepSeek-AI, 2026; Xiaomi, 2026; GLM, 2026). On-policy context distillation further shows that a
context-conditioned teacher can supervise the student’s own trajectories, enabling explicit contextual
knowledge or behavioral criteria to be internalized in the student’s parameters (Ye et al., 2026; Zhao
et al., 2026; H¨ubotter et al., 2026). Together, these developments raise a pivotal question for GRMs:
Can OPD be adapted to consolidate domain-specialized reward teachers into one generalist GRM
while attaining the comparable multi-domain evaluation capability?
To address this question, we introduce RM-OPD (Reward Modeling via On-Policy Distillation),
a two-stage framework that separates specialist acquisition from capability integration. In the first
stage, independent single-domain GRPO training produces domain-specialized reward teachers,
allowing each capability to develop without cross-domain interference. In the second stage, RM-
OPD consolidates these teachers into one student model through three coordinated components: (1)
routed on-policy distillation, which sends each student-generated critique to its matched teacher and
converts teacher–student log-probability differences into dense signed token-level supervision; (2)
domain contribution reweighting (DCR), which aligns each domain’s update share with its target
allocation and prevents any domain from dominating optimization; and (3) optional criteria context
distillation (CCD), which conditions teachers on domain-specific evaluation criteria to sharpen
token-level supervision. Together, these components replace joint sparse-reward optimization with
routed dense supervision that integrates diverse evaluation capabilities into one generalist GRM.
Figure 1 summarizes the faster convergence and stronger capability coexistence achieved by RM-
OPD. Across eight benchmarks and two base families, RM-OPD consistently improves the macro
average over matched Mix-RL by by 4.60–5.55% relative. Our contributions are threefold:
• Limitation analysis of Mixed-domain RL. We identify two structural limitations of Mix-RL for
building generalist GRMs as cross-domain interference and sparse outcome rewards—and show
that Mix-RL leaves a consistent performance gap to independently trained specialists.

RELATED WORK
Reinforcement Learning for Generative Reward Models.
Generative reward models (GenRMs)
cast preference evaluation as autoregressive next-token prediction, producing rubrics, critiques, and
verdicts in natural language (Ye et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025a; Mahan et al., 2024; Zheng et al.,
2023; Yu et al., 2025c). To improve judgment quality beyond supervised fine-tuning, recent work
applies reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) to optimize GenRMs against verdict
correctness, exemplified by GRPO on chain-of-rubrics judgments (Chen et al., 2026), rule-based RL
with inference-time voting (Liu et al., 2025b), and rationale-augmented variants (Wang et al., 2026b;a;
Yu et al., 2025b). However, such RLVR-based training relies on sparse verdict-only rewards and a
single generalist judge, which struggle to supervise intermediate reasoning tokens and to reconcile
heterogeneous domain criteria (Wang et al., 2026b;a). RM-OPD instead delivers dense token-level
supervision from domain-specialized experts on the student’s own judgment rollouts.
On-Policy Distillation.
Unlike classical offline distillation that suffers from distribution shifts and
exposure bias (Kim & Rush, 2016), on-policy distillation (OPD) optimizes the student over its own
generated rollouts, guided by teacher-scored log-probabilities (Li et al., 2026). This paradigm has
proven effective in the LLM domain through reverse-KL and policy-gradient formulations (Agarwal
et al., 2024; Gu et al., 2024), KL-constrained RL unification (Yang et al., 2026b;a), and efficiency-
oriented variants (Kang et al., 2026). The paradigm has since been extended to multi-teacher
routing (Ma et al., 2026; Gao et al., 2026), flow-matching generators (Fang et al., 2026), speculative
drafters (Lei et al., 2026), and self-evaluation (Zhang et al., 2026). Despite this progress, to the best
of our knowledge, no prior work has systematically applied OPD to reward modeling. RM-OPD
fills this gap as the first multi-teacher OPD framework for generative reward models, consolidating
domain-specialized experts into a single generalist GRM.

On-policy distillation.
Knowledge distillation aims to compress teacher capabilities into a student
model by minimizing their output divergence. To mitigate distribution shift, on-policy distillation
(OPD) performs this alignment on student-generated trajectories, obtaining teacher feedback at
prefixes encountered during autoregressive generation (Agarwal et al., 2024). For generative reward
models, the student rθ generates j ∼rθ(· | z), while a frozen teacher rT evaluates the same judgment

4.2
HOW TO RAISE THE COEXISTENCE CEILING?
The analysis above identifies the supervision needed to raise the coexistence ceiling. Raising the
coexistence ceiling requires supervision that is on-policy, covering states induced by the current
student; domain-specific, preserving coherent evaluation criteria; and process-dense, reaching the
critique rather than only the final verdict.
OPD matches these requirements by sampling trajectories from the current student and evaluating
teacher distributions at the visited prefixes (Agarwal et al., 2024; Gu et al., 2024). It preserves
on-policy exploration while replacing outcome rewards with token-level guidance over the judgment
trajectory. Independent domain experts can therefore provide coherent supervision without directly
mixing their sparse rewards.

multi-domain capabilities through routing-based Multi-teacher OPD (MOPD) (Ma et al., 2026).

EXPERIMENTS
To evaluate how effectively RM-OPD consolidates heterogeneous evaluation capabilities into a single
GRM, we conduct experiments across three distinct domains: safety, mathematics, and code. These
domains require distinct evaluation criteria (intent-sensitive judgment, step-level logical verification,
and execution-grounded edge-case analysis), providing a rigorous testbed for assessing cross-domain
capability integration. By benchmarking RM-OPD against independently trained domain specialists,
standard Mix-RL, and strong open-source GRMs, we aim to demonstrate its capacity to mitigate
multi-domain performance trade-offs and improve capability coexistence.

EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
Our training corpus contains 74k cleaned pairwise preferences, organized into mathematics, code,
and safety datasets. The three domain datasets are constructed primarily from the source corpora
used by RM-NLHF (Wang et al., 2026b), together with Themis-CodePreference (Paul et al., 2026);
their full composition is provided in Appendix B.2. For both Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (Yang et al.,
2024) and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (Guo et al., 2025a), we first train one domain-specialized
reward teacher on each dataset using single-domain GRPO (Shao et al., 2024). We then concatenate
and shuffle the three datasets into a common mixed-domain training pool shared by RM-OPD and
MixRL-GRPO. Training is performed on 2 nodes with 8 NVIDIA H800 GPUs each, while evaluation
uses a single 8-GPU H800 node.
We evaluate RM-OPD against four categories of baselines: (1) Base Models: the prompted backbone
models prior to reward-specific fine-tuning; (2) Domain-Specialized RL Teachers: models trained
exclusively on a single domain via GRPO, establishing the in-domain capability ceiling; (3) Mix-RL
(MixRL-GRPO): a generalist GRM optimized via joint GRPO on the combined multi-domain
dataset, representing the prevailing capability integration paradigm; and (4) Existing Open-Source
GRMs: a broad set of strong public reward models, including JudgeLRM (Chen et al., 2025),
CompassJudger-2 (Zhang et al., 2025b), RISE-Judge (Yu et al., 2025a), RM-NLHF (Wang et al.,
2026b), RM-R1 (Chen et al., 2026), and RRM (Guo et al., 2025b). Our evaluation spans eight public
benchmark suites and 16 domain-specific subsets. Appendix B provides the full data composition,
training configuration, baseline specifications, and evaluation protocol.
6.2
MAIN RESULTS
Table 2 reports the primary comparison on RM-Bench, RewardBench, RewardBench 2, and
JudgeBench. Additional results across all 16 benchmark subsets are provided in Appendix Ta-
ble 7. These results reveal two notable observations of RM-OPD.
RM-OPD mitigates the performance limitations of Mix-RL. A common bottleneck in multi-
domain GRMs is that Mix-RL typically underperforms independently trained specialists due to
gradient conflicts (the seesaw effect). Our results demonstrate that RM-OPD effectively addresses
this issue. Across both Qwen2.5 and DeepSeek-R1-Distill bases, MixRL-GRPO consistently lags
7


behind the single-domain RL Teachers (e.g., showing noticeable gaps on RewardBench 2 and
JudgeBench). In contrast, RM-OPD reliably matches the teachers or narrows the gap across domains.
Notably, RM-OPD exhibits a teacher-surpassing effect on complex reasoning tasks: it outperforms
the specialized teacher on RewardBench 2 and RM-Bench Safety. This validates the hypothesis
that separating capability acquisition from integration via on-policy distillation mitigates mutual
degradation and facilitates positive cross-domain synergy.
RM-OPD consistently outperforms existing mixed-domain GRM baselines. Beyond matching
specialist ceilings, RM-OPD demonstrates empirical advantages over other generalist GRMs built by
mixed-domain training. When controlling for the base model family, DeepSeek RM-OPD improves
the overall macro average across all four core benchmarks compared to the matched RM-R1 and
RM-NLHF variants. Furthermore, as detailed in Appendix Table 7, Qwen RM-OPD achieves an
average accuracy of 75.41 across all 16 subsets, outperforming existing open-source GRMs, including
RISE-Judge (74.12), RM-NLHF (71.76), RM-R1 (71.22), and RRM (68.88). The fact that RM-OPD
achieves these results without scaling up model size or data volume indicates that the improvements
are primarily attributed to the architectural design of routed expert distillation over standard joint
multi-domain optimization.

ANALYSIS
To clarify the efficacy of RM-OPD and unveil the limitations of Mix-RL, we now conduct a granular
analysis to isolate its underlying mechanisms. Specifically, we investigate how it breaks the multi-
domain coexistence bottleneck, quantify the contributions of individual components, and evaluate
its out-of-domain generalization. Unless noted otherwise, all analysis utilizes DeepSeek-R1-Distill-
Qwen-7B, supplemented by qualitative case studies in Appendix E.

CONCLUSION
We identified the structural limitations of Mixed-domain Reinforcement Learning (Mix-RL) in
training general-purpose Generative Reward Models (GRMs), where sparse rewards and cross-
domain interference constrain the attainable performance ceiling. To address these bottlenecks, we
introduced RM-OPD, a two-stage framework that decouples domain specialist acquisition from
9


capability integration for GRMs. By employing routed on-policy distillation, domain contribution
reweighting, and criteria context distillation, RM-OPD provides dense, token-level supervision
to effectively consolidate heterogeneous evaluation capabilities into a generalist GRM. Extensive
experiments demonstrate that RM-OPD consistently outperforms Mix-RL baselines and exhibits an
emergent teacher-surpassing effect across diverse domains. Ultimately, our work establishes a highly
effective and promising paradigm for developing robust generalist reward models.

C
LIMITATIONS
While RM-OPD effectively consolidates heterogeneous evaluation capabilities, it presents several
limitations. First, due to computational constraints, our current experiments are restricted to 7B-
parameter models. Although RM-OPD demonstrates strong capability integration and an emergent
teacher-surpassing effect at this scale, we have yet to validate whether these gains scale predictably
to significantly larger frontier models (e.g., 70B+ parameters), where the base capabilities and
distillation dynamics might differ.
Second, RM-OPD relies on dense token-level supervision, which requires full access to the causal
token probabilities of the specialist teachers. This precludes the use of closed-source API judges that
only expose text or terminal scalar scores. Furthermore, our current implementation assumes that the
teachers and the student share the same tokenizer and judgment format; extending the framework to
heterogeneous, cross-family expert pools would require non-trivial representation or token alignment
strategies.
Third, our framework utilizes a hard routing mechanism based on dataset domain metadata. For
inherently mixed-domain prompts, routing a student rollout to a single expert may cause it to ignore
relevant secondary criteria. Additionally, the student inevitably absorbs the biases of its teachers; when
a specialist is confidently wrong, the dense on-policy supervision compels the student to replicate
the error. Developing soft-routing mechanisms for ambiguous prompts, as well as generalizing this
paradigm to process reward models (PRMs) and multimodal settings, remain important directions for
future work.
