Channel-Level Mask Guidance for Slimmable Image Compression
Chen Chen , Chenggang Wu , Yongbiao Chen , Weidong Qiu , Member, IEEE,
Zhengwei Qi , Member, IEEE, and Haibing Guan , Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—Learned image compression (LIC) has achieved
promising rate-distortion performance, but most methods lack
scalability for diverse platforms with varying computational and
storage constraints. Existing slimmable LIC methods achieve
flexible complexity via consecutive-channel activation, but suffer
from two critical limitations. First, forced consecutive-channel
selection degrades narrow-model performance at low bitrates.
Second, the lack of stable variable-rate control limits their
adaptability to varying bandwidth conditions.
To address these limitations, we propose a channel-level
mask-guided slimmable image compression method. A learnable
channel-wise mask with sparsity-promoting regularization en-
ables non-contiguous top-w channel selection, allowing narrow
models to use the most informative channels. After training,
mask merging and channel reordering convert the model into
a standard slimmable network via Mask-to-Slimmable (M2S)
conversion, supporting efficient inference via channel pruning
without architectural changes. For variable-rate control, we
combine λ- and width-conditioned modulation with quantization-
step scaling and a progressive training pipeline stabilized by a
freeze-then-fine-tune strategy.
Experiments on Kodak across E2E, Hyperprior, and Checker-
board backbones show that our method achieves superior rate-
distortion performance across multiple widths and bitrates, with
significant gains for narrow models at low bitrates. At the
narrowest width (w = 48), it achieves up to 19.22%, 24.11%, and
22.76% BD-rate savings over the same-width Slimmable baseline
on the E2E, Hyperprior, and CKBD backbones, respectively. A
single VBR model replaces 40 independent models, reducing
storage by 14.8×–15.9×. Compared with existing elastic LIC
methods, our method outperforms the same-width Slimmable
baseline and compares favorably in terms of rate-distortion
performance, parameter efficiency, and latency flexibility. The
code is available at https://github.com/sjtu-tcloud/MGSIC.
Index Terms—Neural Networks, Image Compression, Scalable
Coding, Variable Bitrate.

As shown in Fig. 1, elastic LIC methods fall into two cate-
gories: spatial scalability and quality scalability. Spatial scal-
ability downsamples the input at the encoder and upsamples
the decoded frame at the decoder [18], [19], [20], [21], often
incorporating side information to enhance quality [20], [22].
Quality scalability further splits into learning-based scalable
coding [13], [14], [6], [23] and slimmable coding [10], [15],
[16], [17], [24]. Among these styles, slimmable coding offers
the best flexibility-efficiency trade-off, yet suffers from two
critical limitations. First, forced consecutive-channel selection
severely degrades narrow-model performance at low bitrates.
Second, existing solutions lack effective variable-rate control,
limiting their adaptability to varying bandwidth conditions.
Variable-rate control is essential for practical codecs. Ex-
isting approaches typically introduce modulation networks
conditioned on λ [25], [26], [27], but they mostly target fixed-
width models. Integrating them into a slimmable framework is
nontrivial: naive λ-sampling fails to decouple width adaptation
from rate modulation, causing training instability.
To address these challenges, we propose a channel-level
mask-guided slimmable image compression method. A learn-
able structured-sparsity mask enables non-contiguous top-w
selection so that narrow models retain the most informative
channels. Mask merging and channel reordering then convert
the mask model into a standard slimmable network (M2S) for
channel-pruned inference without architectural changes. For
variable-rate control, we combine λ- and width-conditioned
modulation with quantization-step scaling and a progressive
freeze-then-fine-tune strategy to decouple width adaptation
from rate modulation.

II. BACKGROUND AND MOTIVATION
A. Learned Image Compression
Three representative LIC models are reviewed: E2E model
[1], Hyperprior model [2], and Context model [3].
E2E Model: an end-to-end optimized model is proposed with
learnable nonlinear analysis/synthesis transforms ga/gs and
arithmetic coding, jointly optimizing rate and distortion [1].
As shown in Fig. 2, input x ∈RH×W ×3 is converted into
latent y by ga. Quantization (Q) casts y into integer ˆy, which is
compressed by arithmetic encoding (AE). Arithmetic decoding
(AD) restores ˆy, and ˆx is reconstructed by gs:
y = ga(x),
ˆy = Q(y),
ˆx = gs(ˆy)
(1)
Usually, uniform noise is used for quantization during training,
and hard rounding during inference. The loss function is:
L = R + λD
(2)
where R is the bitrate (bit per pixel, bpp), D is the distortion
(e.g., MSE), and λ controls the rate-distortion trade-off. Its
factorized entropy model (FEM) assumes i.i.d. elements within
each channel, so it learns one distribution per channel and
requires no per-element µ or σ.
Hyperprior Model: the hyperprior architecture is introduced,
adding a side branch to capture spatial dependencies in y
[2]. As shown in Fig. 2, y undergoes hyper analysis/synthesis
transforms ha/hs to estimate per-element mean µ and scale σ
for y, forming a Gaussian conditional entropy model. The side
information z between ha and hs is quantized and compressed:
z = ha(y),
ˆz = Q(z),
(µ, σ) = hs(ˆz)
(3)
The loss extends Eq. 2 with a side-information rate term:
L = R + Rside + λD
(4)
where Rside is the bitrate of ˆz.
Context Model: Minnen et al. [3] proposed a context model
with autoregressive priors. Its context module gcm is usually
a masked convolution:
gcm(x) = (Mctx ⊙W) × x + b
(5)
where ⊙is the Hadamard product and 0-1 mask Mctx ensures
only decoded pixels (left and above in Z-order) are visible.

CAE, which jointly trains multiple width sub-networks, each
assigned its own RD trade-off λw for the target width. For
target widths W = {w1, w2, . . . , wm}, the objective is:

C. Variable Rate and Complexity Trade-off
Variable-rate methods adapt a single model to varying RD
trade-offs. JPEG scales quantization tables, whereas standard
LIC trains a separate network per λ [1]. Recent methods
condition a single model on λ or bin size [25], [26], [27],
[30], [31], [32], [33], [34], [35] at four locations (Fig. 2): ①
feature maps, ②latent y, ③latent z, and ④the quantizer.
Table I summarizes representative methods. QVRF [25] and
SF [35] use fixed λmax, while others sample larger λ more
often [32], [33]. AG-VAE [30] and DCVC [31] scale only
latent y, whereas MAE [26] and DM [34] modulate interme-
diate features. QVRF uses a scalar regulator, while others use
channel-wise vectors. SF freezes the main model and updates
only scaling factors, degrading low-bitrate performance.
Existing variable-rate methods target fixed-width models,
and integrating them into a slimmable framework couples
width and rate training, causing instability. This is because
λ and width are intrinsically coupled: larger λ requires wider
models, while narrow models saturate at high bitrates [2], [4].
Since low bitrates also cause widths to converge, we decouple
width adaptation from rate modulation.
D. Structured Pruning and Mask-based Channel Selection
Structured pruning removes channels for standard-hardware
deployment [36], while dynamic pruning prunes activations
per input but requires customized hardware [37]. Yin et al. [38]
learn binary channel-importance masks and prune at inference;
EVC [6] sparsifies learnable masks between convolutions and
merges them into adjacent layers. However, these methods
target fixed-width or static pruning and do not support non-
contiguous selection at target widths or joint width/rate adap-
tation, motivating our mask-guided slimmable framework.

A. Overall Architecture
Fig. 4 shows the pipeline with two stages connected by an
M2S conversion. (1) Mask training: a Mask model is trained
with learnable masks and sliced operators (Sec. III-B). The
masks evaluate channel importance and enable non-contiguous
selection. The transforms ga/gs contain mask layers, while ha,
hs, gcm, and gep use sliced operators, and Concat is replaced
by channel shuffling (Sec. III-C). (2) M2S conversion: mask
merging and channel reordering convert the Mask model into
a standard Slimmable network without altering the forward
pass (Fig. 8; Sec. III-D). This allows fine-tuning to mitigate
the sparsity regularization cost and makes active channels con-
tiguous; modules with only sliced operators are reordered by
their adjacent mask layers. (3) VBR training and inference:
the converted model is fine-tuned and deployed for variable-
rate and width-adaptive compression (Sec. III-E). The λ- and
width-conditioned modulation networks generate combined
variables via Hadamard product, applied to ga/gs features
and quantization step sizes for y and z, and channel shuffling
aligns (Φ, Ψ) across widths. A progressive multi-stage strategy
enables one model to cover multiple rate-distortion trade-offs.

C. Channel Shuffling
In the Checkerboard model [8], the latent y ∈RCy×Hy×Wy

IV. EVALUATION
A. Experimental Setup
Datasets. We train on CLIC 2020 and DIV2K HR1 (2357
images), randomly cropping 256×256 patches with batch size
8. For the models compared in Section IV-D, we additionally
include the Flickr 2W dataset (20745 images) during the
width-based modulation fine-tuning stage. CLIC 2021 pro-
fessional validation2 is used for validation, and Kodak (24
images, 768 × 512) for evaluation. Additional test sets (e.g.,
Tecnick) are not included due to space constraints; results can
be added in a revision if requested.
Compared Methods. We compare with (1) traditional codecs:
JPEG, JPEG2000, BPG, and VVC (VTM 19.2) intra-mode;
and (2) elastic LIC methods: CBANet [15], EVC [6], and
ELFIC [16], using publicly released pretrained models. All
models are evaluated in PyTorch 2.6.0 with CompressAI 1.2.8.
Implementation Details. The model is trained and evaluated
on this predefined width set W = {48, 72, 96, 144, 192};
1https://data.vision.ee.ethz.ch/cvl/DIV2K/
2https://archive.compression.cc/2021/tasks/index.html
extending to other widths would require including them in W
during training or fine-tuning, without architectural changes.
We use Adam with initial learning rates 10−4 (main net-
work) and 10−6 (auxiliary entropy parameters), and Λ =
{18, 35, 67, 130, 250, 483, 932, 1800}×10−4. Training runs for
800 epochs until convergence, using ReduceLROnPlateau
(factor 0.5, patience 10); convergence is reached when the
learning rate decays to 1.22 × 10−8. Single-λ models use
a fixed λ ∈Λ. Variable-rate models adopt the progressive
training strategy in Section III-E. Freeze-then-fine-tune vari-
ants (e.g., VBR-QF) use a 100-epoch warm-up in Stage 2
(λ-modulation), and VBR-QFW additionally uses a 100-epoch
warm-up in Stage 3 (w-modulation). For the models compared
in Section IV-D, the Stage 3 warm-up is reduced to 5 epochs.
Hardware. Training: Intel Xeon Platinum 8457C + Nvidia
A100 80GiB. Evaluation: Intel Xeon 48-core E5-2650 +
Nvidia RTX3080 10GiB.
Metrics. We report bitrate (bpp), PSNR (dB), and MS-SSIM3
[40]. RD curves compare methods, with BD-rate [41] as
average bitrate saving at equivalent quality.
B. Slimmable vs Mask vs M2S
We compare three variants: Slimmable model, Mask
model, and M2S model (in Sec. III-D). All variants follow
the training protocol in Sec. IV-A and share the same width
set W. We evaluate E2E, Hyperprior, and CKBD backbones
to demonstrate the generality of our approach.
Narrow widths (w = 48, 72). For all three backbones, Mask
outperforms Slimmable at narrow widths, especially at low
bitrates, confirming that non-contiguous selection preserves
the most informative channels. M2S surpasses both, achieving
the highest PSNR at every bitrate point. At w = 48, M2S
achieves BD-rate savings over Slimmable of 19.22%, 24.11%,
and 22.76% for E2E, Hyperprior, and CKBD, respectively;
over Mask, the savings are 10.54%, 16.24%, and 17.08%.
Fig. 9 shows the RD curves, where M2S dominates both
baselines across all five widths for E2E and Hyperprior, and
across narrow and intermediate widths for CKBD.
Intermediate widths (w = 96, 144). At w = 96, Mask and
Slimmable perform similarly for E2E and Hyperprior, while
CKBD Mask is already slightly worse (+0.36%). At w = 144,
Mask degrades for all three backbones due to regularization
overhead, with CKBD most severe (+11.64%), yet M2S still
recovers to −1.59%. Hyperprior exhibits slightly larger gains
than E2E due to stronger entropy modeling, while CKBD
benefits most from M2S conversion.
Full width (w = 192). Mask underperforms Slimmable
at high bitrates (e.g., ∼0.27 dB at 2.0 bpp for E2E) due
to channel-wise regularization. M2S eliminates this for E2E
and Hyperprior, recovering to −1.29% and −3.59% BD-rate
savings, respectively. For CKBD, M2S reduces the degradation
from +14.11% to +1.06%, i.e., still slightly worse than
Slimmable but far better than Mask (−11.48% vs Mask).
Overall, M2S largely mitigates the regularization overhead
across all three backbones.

w = 192, transform networks cost only 16.2 ms, while
entropy coding dominates: 54.1 ms encoding and 71.7 ms
decoding for y and z. Decoding is higher because Compres-
sAI’s decompress allocates a new GPU tensor per sample,
causing CPU-to-GPU transfer. CDF construction is a one-time
startup cost paid only when λ/w changes, growing from 10.6–
10.7 ms at w = 48 to 106.9–290.5 ms at w = 192, driven
by the λ-dependent z step and amplified by Flickr 2W fine-

and modulation are configuration-dependent and amortized in
steady state, while GPU-side transforms remain stable.
Overall. The modulation network adds only 1.5–4.6% pa-
rameters on CKBD (similar on E2E/Hyperprior), while one
model replaces 40 independent models, cutting storage by
14.8–15.9×. Latency is dominated by entropy coding and CDF
construction, which scale with width and amortize per image.
Since entropy coding scales with active channels, channel
slimming offers a practical latency–RD trade-off in one model.

E. Comparison with Traditional Codecs
We compare our method with four traditional image and
intra-frame compression codecs: JPEG, JPEG2000, BPG, and
VTM 19.2 intra. All codecs are evaluated on the Kodak
dataset, and the quality parameters are selected to cover a
wide bitrate range, as detailed in the supplementary material.
Table VIII summarizes the BPP and PSNR ranges as well
as the average encoding/decoding latency of each traditional
codec. The corresponding rate–distortion curves are shown in
Fig. 12. Among the traditional codecs, VTM achieves the best
rate–distortion performance, but at a high computational cost.
As reported in Table VIII, its average encoding latency is
substantially larger than that of JPEG, JPEG2000, and BPG,
e.g., about 141 s per image for VTM. Our method achieves
clear BD-rate savings over JPEG and JPEG2000, and remains
competitive with BPG, while additionally offering flexible
complexity and continuous rate adaptation. Traditional codecs
run purely on the CPU, whereas LIC methods run transform
networks on the GPU and entropy coding on the CPU.
V. DISCUSSION
Our method focuses on CNN-based VAE image compres-
sion. The proposed mask-guided selection and variable-rate
modulation can extend to attention and Transformer-based
codecs [4], [24]. We use uniform scaling ratios within each
module; future work may explore non-uniform, operator-
level scaling to achieve better balance. Model quantization
is promising for reducing computation and cross-platform
discrepancies [42]. Operator-level redundancy can be further
reduced via efficient transposed convolutions and pruned or
hardware-parallelized context modeling [43]. Since entropy
coding runs serially on the CPU while other components run
on the GPU, it remains the latency bottleneck; future work
may explore parallel entropy coding on CPU/GPU [44], [45].
VI. CONCLUSION
We proposed a channel-level mask-guided slimmable image
compression method for flexible complexity and continuous
variable-rate control in a single model. A learnable channel-
wise mask with sparsity regularization enables non-contiguous
top-w selection, while mask merging and channel reordering
convert the model into a standard slimmable network (M2S)
without altering the forward pass. Variable-rate control com-
bines λ- and width-conditioned modulation with quantization-
step scaling and a progressive freeze-then-fine-tune strategy.
On Kodak, across E2E, Hyperprior, and Checkerboard
backbones, M2S consistently outperforms the same-width
Slimmable baseline, with the largest BD-rate savings at nar-
row widths, while reducing storage over independent models.
VBR-QW is best on E2E and CKBD, and VBR-QFW on
Hyperprior, both approaching the fixed-rate M2S bound. Com-
pared with existing elastic LIC methods, our method offers
a better trade-off among rate–distortion performance, param-
eter efficiency, and latency flexibility. Future work includes
hardware-aware entropy coding and video compression.
